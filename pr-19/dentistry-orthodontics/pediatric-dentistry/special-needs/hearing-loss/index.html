Hearing Loss & Deafness

Great dental care doesn't depend on hearing. As a deaf-friendly dental office, we communicate clearly and visually so deaf and hard-of-hearing children feel informed and at ease.

Visual-first communication

We face your child, use gestures, and show each step before we do it.

Calm and unstartling

We focus on what your child can see and feel, with plenty of reassurance.

Sedation if it helps

Safe options are available when a procedure is hard to tolerate.

Communication That Works for Your Child

We face your child so they can see us, use a tell-show-do approach, and rely on visual cues, gestures, and demonstration to explain each step. We're glad to work with your child's preferred way of communicating and to welcome an interpreter if you bring one.

How We Communicate Clearly

Everything is built around what your child can see and feel.

  1. Face to face We position ourselves so your child can always see us clearly.
  2. Show, then do We demonstrate each tool and step before anything begins.
  3. Visual cues Gestures and demonstration replace spoken instructions.
  4. Interpreters welcome Bring one if you'd like, and we'll work with any method your child prefers.

Why a Visual Approach Works

Because the high-pitched sounds of a dental office aren't part of the experience for many of these children, we focus on what they can see and feel: gentle hands, clear demonstrations, and steady reassurance. When your child can follow along visually, nothing feels startling, and trust comes easily.

When Sedation Can Help

If a procedure is hard to tolerate, safe sedation options are available to keep your child comfortable. We'll recommend the gentlest approach for their needs.

Your child will always know what comes next. We'll show it, and make sure they feel at ease.

Common questions

Can we bring an interpreter?

Absolutely. We welcome interpreters and will work with whatever communication method helps your child understand and feel comfortable.

How do you explain treatment without sound?

We use a tell-show-do approach, showing each tool and demonstrating gently before we begin, so your child always knows what is coming next.

Need to be seen?

Call your office first — most pediatric dentistry are seen the same day.
