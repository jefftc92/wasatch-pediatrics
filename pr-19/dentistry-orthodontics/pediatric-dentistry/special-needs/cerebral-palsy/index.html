Cerebral Palsy

Children with cerebral palsy deserve calm, unhurried dental care that adapts to their needs. Our gentle special needs team takes the time to get every visit right.

Gentle and adaptable

We adapt positioning and pace to your child's movement and comfort.

Supportive and secure

Positioning aids and patient hands help your child feel safe throughout.

Sedation if it helps

Safe options let us provide thorough care gently when needed.

Gentle, Adaptable Care

Movement, muscle tone, and comfort can all shape a dental visit for a child with cerebral palsy. We adapt positioning, use supportive aids, and work patiently so your child feels secure throughout their care.

How We Keep the Visit Comfortable

We take the time to get each detail right, guided by what works for your child.

  1. Supportive positioning We adapt positioning and use aids so your child feels secure and comfortable.
  2. A gentle, patient pace We work slowly and calmly, and never rush a step.
  3. A prevention plan Regular cleanings and a personalized plan protect against reflux, grinding, and decay.
  4. Sedation if needed Safe options let us complete thorough care comfortably.

Why Regular Oral Care Matters

Some children with cerebral palsy are more prone to issues like reflux, difficulty brushing, or grinding. Regular cleanings and a personalized prevention plan help us protect their smile and catch any concerns early, while they're still small and easy to manage.

When Sedation Can Help

When staying comfortable and still is difficult, safe sedation options let us provide thorough care gently. We'll recommend the lightest, safest approach for your child.

We'll take the time to get every detail right, so your child feels secure and their smile stays healthy.

Common questions

How do you position my child comfortably?

We use supportive positioning and adapt to what is most comfortable and secure for your child. Your guidance about what works at home helps us a great deal.

Can sedation help my child relax?

Yes. We offer safe sedation options, including general anesthesia when appropriate, so treatment can be completed comfortably.

Need to be seen?

Call your office first — most pediatric dentistry are seen the same day.
