Fluoride Treatments

A quick, painless fluoride treatment is one of the simplest ways to protect your child's teeth from cavities. Here's how it works, and why it gives young enamel a boost that toothpaste alone can't.

What a Fluoride Treatment Is

A professional fluoride treatment is a quick application of a concentrated, tooth-strengthening varnish or gel. We usually apply it right after a routine cleaning. It takes about a minute, tastes pleasant, and is completely painless.

How We Apply It

It's one of the easiest steps of the whole visit. Here's all there is to it.

1

After a cleaning

We apply it to clean, dry teeth, usually right after the cleaning.

2

Paint it on

We gently brush the varnish onto each tooth.

3

Sets in seconds

It sets quickly, with no mess and nothing to hold in the mouth.

4

Eat & drink soon

Your child can usually get right back to their day.

Why Fluoride Protects Young Teeth

Fluoride strengthens tooth enamel and helps it resist the acids that cause cavities. For children, whose enamel is still developing, a professional treatment offers protection that toothpaste alone can't match, lowering the risk of decay between visits. Paired with sealants and good habits, it's a cornerstone of prevention. The American Dental Association recognizes professionally applied fluoride as a safe, effective way to help prevent tooth decay.

When & Who It's For

We typically apply fluoride at each six-month checkup, and nearly every child benefits. Kids who are more prone to cavities, or who drink mostly bottled water, may benefit from more frequent treatments, which we tailor to your child's needs.

Common questions

Is fluoride safe for my child?

Yes. Professional fluoride treatments use a carefully measured amount that is safe and effective for children. We tailor it to your child's age and needs.

How long does a fluoride treatment take?

Only a minute or two. We brush or paint it on after a cleaning, and your child can usually eat and drink shortly afterward.

How often should my child get fluoride?

Typically at each six-month checkup, though children at higher risk for cavities may benefit from more frequent application.
