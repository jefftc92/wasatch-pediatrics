Children's Dentistry

Pulp Therapy

Often called a baby root canal, pulp therapy treats the inside of an infected tooth when decay or injury reaches the nerve, relieving your child's pain and saving the tooth.

What Pulp Therapy Is

When a cavity or injury reaches the soft inner pulp of a tooth, it can cause pain, sensitivity, and infection. Pulp therapy removes the affected tissue, disinfects the tooth, and seals it, usually followed by a crown to protect it.

How We Keep It Comfortable

It sounds intensive, but for your child it's a calm, comfortable visit.

1

We completely numb the tooth so there's nothing to feel.

2

3

Comfort options available

For anxious children, safe sedation options make the visit even easier.

Why Saving the Tooth Matters

Treating the tooth instead of removing it keeps your child's bite intact, preserves space for the adult tooth, and avoids the discomfort of an untreated infection. It's the gentlest way to relieve pain while protecting your child's developing smile.

When & Who It's For

Ongoing tooth pain, sensitivity to hot or cold, or swelling can be signs that decay has reached the nerve. If your child has these symptoms, our toothache care can help right away. Children with a deep cavity or a dental injury that has reached the nerve are the typical candidates, on both baby teeth and young permanent teeth.

We'll get your child out of pain gently, and save the tooth so their smile keeps developing on track.

Common questions

Is pulp therapy the same as a root canal?

It is similar and often called a baby root canal. It treats the inner pulp of a tooth to relieve pain and infection while saving the tooth.

Will my child be in pain during the procedure?

No. We fully numb the tooth, and most children are surprised by how comfortable it is. Sedation is available for added comfort.

How will I know if my child needs pulp therapy?

Ongoing tooth pain, sensitivity to temperature, or swelling can be signs. We'll examine the tooth and recommend the gentlest effective treatment.
