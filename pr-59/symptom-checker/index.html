Getting your child seen

  • Talk to a nurse now, at any hour. Call your office’s main number, nights, weekends and holidays included. You will reach a nurse or a physician, not an answering service.
  • Be seen today. Every office keeps same-day appointments for illness and injury, from sore throats and rashes through wound care, concussions and the first treatment of a fracture.
  • This evening or at the weekend. Most offices run After Hours Care. Book it rather than turning up; there are no walk-ins, and the hours differ by office.

If you are worried, call. We would rather answer a question that turns out to be nothing than have you sit up all night deciding whether it was.

Common questions

Who answers if I call at night?

A nurse or a physician. We staff an on-call line 24 hours a day, and you reach it through your office’s ordinary main number, so there is no separate number to find.

Do I need an appointment for After Hours Care?

Yes. Call first. After Hours visits are same-day urgent appointments, and we do not take walk-ins.

Does every office run After Hours Care?

Most do, and the hours differ from one office to the next. Check your office’s page for its own hours before you set out.

Should I use the Symptom Checker or just call?

Use whichever settles it faster. The Checker is good at telling you whether something needs a doctor at all. Call us if it says to, or if you are still uneasy afterwards. The Checker cannot hear the cough or ask the next question.

My child is sick and we are not patients yet. Can we be seen?

Call the office nearest you and ask. Registering as a new patient does not have to happen first.
