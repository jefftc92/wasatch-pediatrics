Dental Emergencies

Dental emergencies don't wait for business hours, and neither do we. Call us and we'll get your child comfortable fast, with same-day, after-hours, and teledentistry care.

Seen the same day

We keep room in the schedule for urgent visits, so call and we'll fit your child in.

Help after hours too

After-hours phone support and teledentistry, so you're never left guessing.

Calm, kid-first care

We get your child comfortable first, then fix the problem gently.

What counts as a dental emergency?

If your child has a knocked-out permanent tooth, severe or worsening pain, facial swelling, or heavy bleeding, call us right away. A knocked-out tooth in particular has the best chance of being saved within the first 30 to 60 minutes.

For a chipped tooth, a lost filling, a poking braces wire, or a sore that won't settle, we can usually guide you over the phone and see your child the same day. When in doubt, call. We'd rather hear from you and help you decide.

What to do

  1. Call us first Phone (801) 676-3700 and we'll guide you, day or night.
  2. Simple first aid A cold compress, children's pain reliever, or saving a tooth in milk can make a real difference. We'll tell you exactly what to do.
  3. Come in same-day We reserve time for urgent visits, so your child is seen quickly.
  4. Comfort, then care We ease the pain first, then treat the cause gently and completely.

Common questions

What counts as a dental emergency?

A knocked-out permanent tooth, severe or worsening pain, facial swelling, heavy bleeding, or a dental injury all warrant an immediate call. When in doubt, call. We'd rather hear from you and help you decide.

Can you really see my child today?

We reserve time each day for emergencies and will do everything we can to see your child the same day. Call (801) 676-3700 as early as possible.

What if it happens at night or on a weekend?

We offer after-hours phone support to guide you, and teledentistry visits when appropriate, so you always have somewhere to turn.

When should we go to the ER instead?

For trouble breathing or swallowing, a possible broken jaw, or bleeding that won't stop, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room. For most dental emergencies, call us first.

Need to be seen?

Call your office first — most dental emergencies are seen the same day.
