Toothache & Pain

Tooth Sensitivity

A quick zing with ice cream is common, but sensitivity that keeps coming back is a tooth trying to tell you something. Here's what causes it, and how we make it stop.

All Dental Emergencies

What Causes Sensitive Teeth in Kids?

Sensitivity to hot, cold, or sweet can come from a new cavity, thin or worn enamel, a small crack, gum irritation, or recently erupted teeth. A brief twinge that passes is usually minor, but pain that lingers or worsens deserves a visit.

We find the cause

Cavity, worn enamel, or a small crack, the treatment follows the reason.

Often an easy fix

Many cases resolve with fluoride or a small filling.

Gentle, kid-paced care

A calm look, plain words, and no rushing.

What You Can Do at Home

A few easy changes often calm things down while you watch it.

1

Soft brush, gentle strokes

Hard brushing wears enamel and irritates gums.

2

Fluoride toothpaste

It strengthens enamel and calms sensitivity over time.

3

Skip the extremes

Avoid very hot, cold, or acidic foods for a few days.

4

Watch the calendar

If it lasts beyond a week, or worsens, give us a call.

How We Treat It

We'll find the cause and match the treatment to it, from a fluoride treatment that strengthens enamel to a filling if there's decay. Most sensitivity is very treatable.

Why It's Worth Checking, and When

Ongoing sensitivity is often the earliest warning of a cavity or a crack, exactly the stage where the fix is smallest and easiest. Come in if the sensitivity lasts more than a week, keeps returning, or arrives with a real toothache. Catching it early is the whole game.

A sensitive tooth is a tooth asking for a little help early. Give us a call, and we'll keep the fix small.

Common questions

Is tooth sensitivity an emergency?

Usually not, but lasting or worsening sensitivity can signal a cavity or crack. If it persists beyond a week or comes with pain, have it checked.

Can sensitive teeth be prevented?

Gentle brushing, fluoride, and regular checkups go a long way. We can also apply treatments that strengthen enamel and reduce sensitivity.

Why does cold suddenly bother a new adult tooth?

Newly erupted teeth are often sensitive at first and usually settle down. If it lingers or worsens, we're happy to take a look.
