# Round 2 blind evaluation B (reader) — FAIL

Evaluator: editorial-evaluator-reader, blind to the brief and to evaluator A.
Six pages: fever, croup, diaper-rash, head-injury, suicide-concerns, constipation.

## High severity

### fever
1. "A child who does all three is usually well enough to watch at home, whatever the
   thermometer says." then later "The second is a fever above 104 F."
   Reader: told the number does not matter, then a number matters. Which wins for a
   child who is drinking, playing and reads 104.5?
2. "Three fevers need care right away." — follows a block of 911 and ER items, so the
   reader believed they were already in the right-away list. A new list starts inside
   the same paragraph.
3. The age rules collide. "Call us within a day for any fever in a baby three to six
   months old. Call within a day too for a fever lasting more than three days...
   Between six and 24 months, call within a day if a fever with no other symptom lasts
   more than 48 hours." Reader with a 10-month-old could not tell which rule applied
   after three readings. This is the question the page exists to answer.
4. Paragraph 2 is one unbroken slab of roughly fifteen instructions. Wrong shape for a
   parent standing in a dark hallway with a phone.

### croup
5. "While you listen, run a hot shower in a closed bathroom..." switches from judging
   severity to treatment mid-paragraph. Reader does not know whether to assess or
   steam, and steaming for twenty minutes is wrong if the stridor is severe. The page
   never says "unless it is severe."

### head-injury
6. "Watch closely for the first two hours after the injury, then wake your child after
   two hours of sleep." Two different two-hour clocks in one sentence. Reader could not
   tell whether the second two hours runs from falling asleep. This is the sentence the
   page exists for.

### suicide-concerns
7. "Firearms cause most suicide deaths in North America, which is why they come first."
   The page explains its own ordering to a frightened parent. That clause is the writer
   talking about the article rather than to the reader.
8. "Behavioral health services at our offices are for established patients only, so if
   your child is one..." — "one" what. And the eligibility rule is the last thing on
   the page, reading as small print on a suicide page.

### constipation
9. "a stool softener is the next step, and we would rather go over it with you before
   you start. Miralax... The usual amount is one to three teaspoons of powder a day
   with dinner, each teaspoon mixed into two ounces of water." Call first, but here is
   the full dose anyway. Reader did arithmetic (up to six ounces) and still did not
   know whether the range is by age or weight.

## Medium severity

- fever: "Give that baby no fever medicine before the visit." Nobody talks like this.
  Also needs half a line of reason, because the parent's instinct is the opposite.
- croup: "you hear it on the way in rather than on the cough" — on the way in where.
  Say breathing in, as the lead already does.
- croup: "unless the drooling is from teething" hands a midnight airway judgement to
  the parent before calling 911.
- croup: "For stridor in an older child" — older than one. Reader with a 14-month-old
  had to look back to the previous sentence.
- diaper-rash: "three days of that" twice in consecutive sentences across a paragraph
  break, with the yeast description in between.
- diaper-rash: "An anti-yeast cream such as Lotrimin AF treats it, no prescription
  needed, twice a day." Three things bolted with commas. How many days. Does the
  warm-water care continue alongside.
- diaper-rash: five call/tell-us sentences in a row; "tell us" appears once from
  nowhere. Reader stopped absorbing by the fourth.
- head-injury: "Two things about a lump change the answer" — the answer to what. No

- suicide-concerns: "or is confused in speech or behavior" is vague inside a 911

- suicide-concerns: "Lock away any firearms and make sure they are unloaded, and better

- constipation: "The other, and the most common cause of repeated constipation, is a

- constipation: "Vomiting is the one thing here that can mean an emergency." "Here"

## Pattern across pages

The recurring structural fault is the undifferentiated call-us pile-up: consecutive
sentences that each open with a routing instruction, in one paragraph, with the urgency
tier carried only by an adverb phrase in the middle of the sentence. It appears on
fever, diaper-rash and constipation. Fixing it needs structure, not wording.
