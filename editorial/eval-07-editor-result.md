# Round 7 blind evaluation A (senior editor) — FAIL

## Critical

1. **suicide-concerns, a collision my own precedence device created.** The lead
   ends "If you are frightened and none of those signs fit your child yet, call
   or text 988." Take the child the routing separates: an attempt five days ago.
   No attempt in 24 hours, no current threat, no plan, so none of those signs
   fit, and the lead routes them to a hotline. Paragraph 1 then says go to an
   emergency room tonight. The AAP puts an attempt within the last week at Go To
   ER Now. The scanning parent reaches the less urgent route first. "Those signs"
   also carries a different referent here than in the sentence before it.
2. **diaper-rash, a deleted trigger.** The source gives two independent triggers
   for an anti-yeast cream: "If you've tried this **or the rash is bright red**,
   suspect a yeast infection." The page deletes the bright-red trigger and makes
   three failed days the only one. A baby with an unmistakable yeast rash today,
   which paragraph 1 has just taught the parent to recognize, is told to wait
   three days. It nullifies the page's own paragraph 1.

## Major

3. **croup never names the When To Call tab**, alone among the six. A
   two-year-old working visibly to breathe and breathing fast, with no stridor
   and no retractions, matches no route in our prose, and the page's only
   remaining instruction sends him to warm mist and home care. The omission
   converts into an implicit self-care route, quieter than the source.
4. **head-injury, "those signs... have already passed"** is meant to scope to the
   four in the previous sentence, but a seizure and a loss of consciousness have
   by definition already passed, and both are 911 items four sentences earlier. A
   parent whose child seized and is now settled can read this sentence as hers
   and drive instead of calling 911.
5. **fever, an unbounded reassurance.** "The heat itself is still not damaging
   your child" is attached to the whole band from 104 upward. The AAP's own scale
   says above 106 it is important to bring the fever down and above 108 the fever
   itself can be harmful. Correct at 104 to 106, wrong above it, and the sentence
   draws no line. This is my sentence, written to replace one that cited the AAP
   at the reader.
6. **diaper-rash, "time confirms the answer"** subordinates the appearance test
   to the clock, which is what produces finding 2. Paragraph 1's whole yeast
   description gets the job of "suspect" with no action attached.
7. **constipation, half an item.** The source reads "recent onset of weak suck
   **or weak muscles**." The page keeps the suck and drops the muscles, so a
   floppy infant still feeding normally matches nothing. Rule 12.
8. **suicide-concerns, "Call us at any hour"** carries no condition, sits after
   two emergency-room routes, and reads as an alternative to the ER rather than
   an addition.
