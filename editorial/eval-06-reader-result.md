# Round 6 blind evaluation B (reader) — FAIL

Every page answered the question the reader arrived with. All six "did it tell
me what I came for" answers are yes. The reader also checked fever and croup
against the AAP directly and confirmed both "sit at or above the AAP's urgency
tier, never below."

The failure is three rereads and one structural pattern.

## The structural pattern: a 911 list split across a paragraph break

Reported on fever and croup independently.

- fever: paragraph 1 ends "Call 911 too if your child has stopped moving", and
  paragraph 2 opens "Call 911 as well if your child is struggling for each
  breath". Reader: "At 2am I scan. If I'd stopped at the end of the first
  paragraph, thinking that was the emergency list, I'd have missed the purple
  spots. Nothing about the break tells me more 911 items are coming."
- croup: same shape. "The second paragraph has five more 911 triggers including
  blue lips and drooling. Those are the ones a frightened parent needs first."

A paragraph break reads as the end of a list. Emergency items must not be split
across one.

## The three rereads

1. **head-injury, the duplicated four-item list.** "I thought I'd lost my place
   and scrolled back up. The four items are word for word identical. The whole
   difference is buried in 'right now' versus 'and seems fine now', and one
   sends me to 911 and the other to the ER." Reported in round 5 as well, and
   round 6's fix did not change the shape. Name the four signs once, then split
   by timing.
2. **diaper-rash, the zigzag.** "It bounces yeast, ordinary, exception-to-
   ordinary, yeast again, and then repeats the three-day rule I was given in the
   sentence just before it. Second time through I was checking whether the three
   days was a new number or the same one." The diarrhea ointment aside also
   arrives exactly when the reader is deciding about a cream.
3. **constipation, "Every other child".** "For a beat I read 'every other child'
   as every second child, and then couldn't make 'almost always' agree with it.
   You mean any child who isn't breastfed. Say that."

## Placement

4. **head-injury, "Your child can sleep."** "This is the thing I came here for.
   It's in the last third of the third paragraph, underneath two full paragraphs
   of emergency lists and a physiology explanation. Your own summary at the top
   promises it. At midnight, with a child who bumped their head and is now
   yawning, I want that in the first screen, not the fourth."
