# Revision brief — packet 1, round 2

Both blind evaluators returned FAIL. This carries only what is unresolved.

## First: three findings you should NOT act on

The evaluators were handed a packet that under-described the site, and two of
their critical findings are artifacts of that. Do not "fix" this copy.

**Not a fault.** "you reach a nurse or a physician on call rather than an
answering service". The site says this verbatim: "You will reach a nurse or a
physician, not an answering service." Supported. Keep.

**Not a fault.** "Behavioral health services are available at our offices". The
site says "behavioral health services are available at our Wasatch Pediatric
offices". Supported as written.

**Not a fault.** Four of the six pages saying "call us" without giving a number.
Every symptom page ends with a band carrying the 24/7 nurse line, same-day
visits, After Hours Care and a Find your office link. The intro copy does not
need to repeat it. See the opposite finding at C1.

## A. Facts. Fix first.

**A1. fever: 104F is seek-care-now, not same day.** AAP puts "Fever higher than
104 F" under Call Doctor or Seek Care Now. It currently sits in a same-day
sentence with two genuinely 24-hour items and has been levelled down to match
them. Rule 15.

**A2. fever: a child who stops drinking is seek-care-now.** AAP files suspected
dehydration there. The page says same day, and paragraph 1 already says a child
who refuses every drink "needs to be seen". The page gives two answers to one
question.

**A3. head-injury: "a fall from higher than your child is tall" is invented.**
AAP says "Fall from a dangerous height" and defines nothing. Do not invent a
threshold. Either use the AAP's wording or give one you can source.

**A4. head-injury: "hard to wake up" is missing entirely.** It is a Call 911 Now
item in the source. The paragraph opens by telling a parent to watch how their
child wakes and then never says what a failure to wake means.

**A5. head-injury: neck pain is missing.** AAP puts "Neck pain after head
injury" under Go to ER Now, and "Not moving neck normally" under Call 911 Now.
Neither appears on the page.

**A6. head-injury: "overnight" is invented.** AAP's instruction is to watch
closely for two hours. "Overnight" also assumes the injury happened in the
evening. "Exactly" overpromises.

**A7. constipation: "more than a week" is invented.** AAP's trigger is
"infrequent stools do not get better after changes to diet", with no duration.

**A8. suicide-concerns: add the qualification we found.** The site says
behavioral health services "are for established patients only". A parent
arriving in crisis who is not already a patient needs to know that before they
rely on it. 988, 911 and the emergency room are open to everyone; say so.

## B. Logic

**B1. suicide-concerns: "which is why this comes before anything else" is false
on the page's own terms.** The lead puts 988 first and the paragraph opens with
911. "This" also has no clear antecedent. A sentence that appears to rank
means-removal above calling 911 is dangerous here. Rewrite or cut.

**B2. suicide-concerns: the firearms or-chain offers the same action twice.**
"Take firearms out of the house" and "store them with a relative or friend" are
one option, not two, and the AAP ranks them ("Lock away any firearms. Be sure
they are unloaded. Better yet, store them with a relative or friend"). Restore
the ranking.

**B3. diaper-rash: the under-one-month scope is ambiguous.** "Call the same day
too for yellow scabs..." leaves the reader unable to tell whether the age
qualifier still applies. In the source all three are age-conditioned.

**B4. head-injury: "Call us about anything short of that" points at a paragraph
holding six thresholds.** The reader cannot tell what it excludes.

**B5. constipation: "praise and small rewards do more than either" sets the bar
below zero.** Doing more than two things that make the problem worse is not a
claim of benefit.

**B6. head-injury: "Size still has a limit" is cryptic** and only resolves after
the next sentence.

## C. Structure

**C1. fever: cut the routing sentence from paragraph 2.** It repeats the band
below the page verbatim, and it splits a descending triage list in half.
Evaluator B lost its place there. This is the correct action, not adding
routing to the other pages.

**C2. fever: paragraph 2 is eight sentences at three urgency levels in one
block.** Evaluator B read it twice and could not afterwards say which items were
"now" and which were "same day". Break it so the tiers are visible.

**C3. constipation: "Call 911 if your child's vomit is green" is the last line,**
after two less urgent tiers, with nothing before it about vomit. Move it up and
give it a clause of context.

**C4. constipation: paragraph 2 opens on stool softeners** with no bridge from
paragraph 1's toilet-training power struggles.

**C5. constipation: the heading's second half is never answered.** "Why that
keeps constipation going" — the page asserts the loop and never explains the
mechanism, which is the one thing the panel does not do.

## D. Questions the pages raise and do not answer

**D1. head-injury: can my child sleep?** This is the question a parent opens the
page at night to ask. The page says "we will tell you exactly what to watch for
overnight", which reads as withholding the answer until they phone. The AAP
source answers it. So should we.

**D2. croup: what do I do in the next ten minutes** if this is not an emergency?
The page identifies stridor and never says what to do about a child barking in
the next room right now.
