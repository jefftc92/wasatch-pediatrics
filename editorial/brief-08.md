# Revision brief 08 — six symptom pages

**Process change.** Your draft now goes to three checkers before it is applied:
editorial-triage, editorial-structure and editorial-panel. Read
EDITORIAL_CHECKLIST.md. Rules 17 to 21 finally have owners, and the nine
standing questions at the foot of that file are what you will be checked
against, so answer them yourself first.

Three of the faults below are mine, introduced in round 7 while fixing round 6.

## Critical

1. **suicide-concerns, a collision created by the precedence device.** The lead
   ends "If you are frightened and none of those signs fit your child yet, call
   or text 988." A child who attempted suicide five days ago fits none of the
   lead's signs, so the lead routes them to a hotline while paragraph 1 routes
   them to an emergency room tonight, which is where the AAP puts an attempt
   within the last week. The scanning parent reaches the softer route first. The
   lead must carry all three routes with no gap between them, or the ER window
   must move into the lead. "Those signs" also shifts referent between two
   consecutive sentences.
2. **diaper-rash, a deleted trigger.** The AAP gives two independent triggers for
   an anti-yeast cream: "If you've tried this **or the rash is bright red**,
   suspect a yeast infection." We kept only the three-day one. A baby with an
   obvious yeast rash today, exactly the baby paragraph 1 teaches the parent to
   recognize, is told to wait three days. Restore the appearance trigger, and
   stop subordinating the look test to the clock. Paragraph 1's description must
   lead somewhere.

## Major

3. **fever, an unbounded reassurance, and it is my sentence.** "The heat itself
   is still not damaging your child" is attached to everything from 104 upward.
   The AAP's own scale says above 106 it is important to bring the fever down,
   and above 108 the fever itself can be harmful. Bound it or cut it.
4. **croup never names the When To Call tab**, alone among the six pages. A
   two-year-old working visibly to breathe and breathing fast, with no stridor
   and no retractions, matches no route in our prose and is left with warm mist
   and home care. That is quieter than the source. Name the tab, and make sure
   that child has somewhere to go.
5. **head-injury, a timing split that does not hold.** "If any of those signs
   came after the injury and have already passed, drive to an emergency room
   instead." A seizure and a loss of consciousness have by definition already
   passed, and both are 911 items four sentences earlier. Fence the split so it
   cannot reach them.
6. **constipation, half an item.** The source says "recent onset of weak suck
   **or weak muscles**." We dropped the muscles, so a floppy infant who is still
   feeding normally matches nothing.
7. **suicide-concerns, "Call us at any hour"** has no condition and sits after
   two ER routes, so it reads as an alternative to the ER rather than an
   addition. Paragraph 3 already carries the call route.
8. **head-injury, the missing age rule.** The AAP puts "age less than 1 year old"
   in the same call-now tier as the one-inch swelling and the skull dent, both of
   which the page carries. A reader with a seven-month-old read the page's
   silence as proof age does not matter here. Rule 21.

## Reader findings

9. **fever, the forehead contradiction.** The page says a forehead reading counts
   and then says forehead readings are not reliable. The source's word is
   **digital**: paper strips are what fail. A parent with a digital forehead
   thermometer was left unsure whether to trust the reading in their hand. Also
   say what to use instead under 6 months.
10. **fever, the definition line** puts five places and three numbers in one line
    of prose. "My eye kept sliding off."
11. **fever placement.** The under-3-months rule, which the meta description
    leads with, sits in the last quarter of paragraph 2 behind ten emergency
    signs.
12. **constipation gives nothing to do tonight.** "Croup names the hot shower.
    Diaper rash names Lotrimin. This page names nothing." One food and one
    sentence about not making the child sit there would close it.
13. **croup, after the twenty minutes.** The mist ends and the page has nothing.
    The reader worked out they should listen again, in a steamy bathroom, by
    scrolling back up.
14. **croup never says what ordinary croup looks like.** A barking cough with no
    stridor. "I inferred I was fine. I would rather have been told."
15. **constipation, "goes four or five days"** is missing "without a stool".
16. **constipation, "that pain is the reason to hold on again"** first reads as
    advice that there is a reason to hold on. It is the central idea of the page.
17. **croup, a child aged exactly one year** falls between "under a year" and
    "over a year".
18. **head-injury, "mean 911 while your child has them"** reads as a condition
    attached to the wrong thing.
19. **diaper-rash, "with one exception"** promises a rash cleaning does not fix
    and delivers a rash that also needs ointment. An addition, not an exception.
20. **suicide-concerns, "need attention now"** is the only soft phrase on an
    otherwise concrete page.

## Minor

- fever: "Any of those readings counts" restates the previous sentence and is
  contradicted inside its own sentence.
- fever: "a baby under 3 months old, which is 12 weeks" attaches the clause to
  the baby rather than the age.
- croup: "a face turning blue" takes the wrong article for the child's own face.
- croup: paragraph 1 opens by restating the lead.
- diaper-rash: "Its red is bright" where "bright red" would not have paused the
  reader. Three days stated twice in consecutive sentences.
- head-injury: "calling your office's main number" verbatim in adjacent
  sentences, and longer than the "call us now, at any hour" the other pages use.
- constipation: an exception, then an exception to the exception, with four
  different day counts in one paragraph.
