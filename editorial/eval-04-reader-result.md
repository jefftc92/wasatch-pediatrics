# Round 4 blind evaluation B (reader) — FAIL

The failure is concentrated on one page. Head-injury paragraph 3 was named "the
best writing in the set."

## Croup, which is what makes this a FAIL

1. **Blue lips appears in two tiers, four sentences apart.** "Bluish lips or a
   bluish face while your child is not coughing means 911" and then "Lips that
   turn blue during a coughing fit are not safe either... Call your office's main
   number." Reader: "I understand it now, but I had to stop and decode it, and I
   would not have managed that while watching it happen."
2. **"either sign" cannot be resolved at reading speed.** "Not safe either" reads
   as more emergency room, because everything above it is emergency room, and
   then the sentence routes to a phone call. "I had to count backwards to work
   out which two things either sign points at. Standing in a bathroom with a
   barking child, I would get this wrong."
3. **Severe croup is followed immediately by an instruction to run a shower.**
   "You just told me my child has severe croup and then, in the next breath, told
   me to go run a shower for twenty minutes. Is a shower the right response to
   severe?" The answer is in the last line of the next paragraph.
4. **The cold-air step is withheld at the moment it is needed.** The page gives
   the shower method in full and then points at a tab for what to do when the
   shower fails. "It's 20 minutes later, the mist didn't work, and now you're
   sending me to a tab."

## Fever

5. **"whatever the number says" with no ceiling.** "My kid is 104.5. Really, any
   number? I don't believe you and I don't know if you mean it, so now I'm
   scrolling for a ceiling number and the page never gives me one." Rule 18 was
   satisfied by defining a fever and not by answering whether a high number is
   itself dangerous.
6. **The thermometer reason only rules out ears.** "I own a forehead thermometer,
   not an ear one... Two sentences earlier you told me forehead readings are
   valid at 100.4, so now I'm holding two half-answers together."
7. Paragraph 1 goes calm, definition, thermometer technique, 911. "I had to reset
   twice inside one block of text."

## Diaper rash

8. **Two methods of telling the rashes apart, unreconciled.** Paragraph 1 says
   judge by looking, paragraph 2 opens "Time tells you which rash your baby has."
9. **No cream is named.** "I'm going to a drugstore shelf with about nine tubes on
   it and this sentence doesn't name a single one." Also "over the counter
   without a prescription" says the same thing twice.

## Head injury

10. The four signs repeated in consecutive paragraphs "looks like a copy-paste
    error until you work it out." The distinction is now clear but the shape is
    not. This is the fix for round 3's tier ambiguity, so the content is right
    and the presentation needs work, not reversal.
11. "Four more signs mean 911" counts the signs out loud. "Reads like something
    assembled rather than said."
12. "about 2.5 centimeters" — "I'm in Utah. I did not need the centimeters."
13. "a cut deep enough to need many stitches" asks the parent to make the
    judgement that is the reason they would be going in.

## Suicide concerns

14. "attempted suicide in the past 24 hours, or is acting or talking confused"
    puts two unlike things side by side. "I had to guess you mean she's taken
    something. If that's what it means, say it."
15. "which is why they come before everything else on this list" — the paragraph
    is not a list, and the reader glanced up looking for bullets.
16. "established patient" is admin language one sentence after the most
    frightening instruction on the page.

## Constipation

17. "Green vomit is the rare exception" announces the exception before the rule.
18. "That exception is about how often a stool comes and nothing else" explains
    an explanation the sentence before already made.

## Verdicts by page

- diaper-rash: partly. Can tell the rashes apart, does not know what to buy.
- head-injury: **yes.** "Paragraph 3 is the best writing in the set."
- suicide-concerns: **yes.**
