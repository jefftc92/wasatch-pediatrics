# Revision 05 — six symptom pages

### fever
Meta description: Most childhood fevers come from ordinary viruses. What counts as a fever, which fevers mean 911 or an emergency room, and why a fever in a baby under 3 months means calling us tonight.
Paragraph 1: Three questions settle most fevers at home. Is your child drinking? Do they respond to you normally? Do they play a little once fever medicine has taken the edge off? A child who does all three is usually well enough to watch at home, even at 103. A high number is uncomfortable rather than dangerous, and the fever itself is not damaging your child. Above 104°F, call us the same day even if your child seems well. A true fever means 100.4°F or higher taken rectally, in the ear or on the forehead, 100°F by mouth, or 99°F under the arm. Any of those readings counts, though an ear thermometer is not reliable under 6 months, and forehead strips are not reliable at any age. Call 911 for a child who cannot be woken, or who has stopped moving or is too weak to stand, even when the temperature itself looks ordinary.
Paragraph 2: Call 911 as well if your child is struggling for each breath and can barely speak or cry, or if purple or blood-colored spots appear on the skin. An emergency room is the right place for a stiff neck or for a seizure during the fever. Go as well if your baby is under a year old and the soft spot on the head bulges or swells. Take your child there too if they are hard to wake, are not alert when awake, or act or talk confused. Any fever in a baby under 3 months old, which is 12 weeks, means calling us at any hour, because at that age we check rather than wait. Do not give a baby under 3 months any fever medicine before we have seen them. The When To Call tab below lists the fevers that need a call within a day, and the Care Advice tab covers comfort and dosing.

### croup
Paragraph 1: Stridor is the sound to listen for, and crying makes it louder, so comfort your child first and listen again once they have settled. Stridor you can still hear while your child is calm and resting means severe croup, and severe croup needs to be seen tonight rather than treated at home. For milder stridor, start warm mist. Run a hot shower in a closed bathroom and sit with your child in the mist for twenty minutes, staying away from the water so that nobody gets burned. If the mist has not helped by then and the night is cold, take your child outside for a few minutes. Call us either way, because stridor means your child needs to be seen rather than advised over the phone.
Paragraph 2: Call 911 if your child is struggling for each breath, if the stridor is constant and severe, or if your child passes out or stops breathing. Call 911 for lips or a face turning blue, whether or not your child is coughing at the time. Drooling, spitting or great trouble swallowing means 911, and so does croup that started suddenly after a bee sting, a new medicine, or a food your child is allergic to. Go straight to an emergency room if your baby is under a year old and has stridor. Go as well if the ribs pull in with each breath, or if your child may have choked on a small object. In a child over a year old, stridor you can hear right now means calling your office's main number at any hour.

### diaper-rash
Paragraph 1: Ordinary diaper rash comes from stool sitting against the skin, so wetness on its own is rarely the problem. Getting the stool off the skin at every change is most of the treatment, and the Care Advice tab below gives the method. A yeast rash looks different. Its red is bright, and its border is a sharp line rather than a fade. The skin inside that border can look raw and weepy, and separate red spots or small pimples sit just beyond the border.
Paragraph 2: The look of the rash tells you which one to suspect, and time confirms the answer. A rash that is no better after three days of warm-water cleaning and air exposure has probably picked up yeast, because yeast does not clear with cleaning alone. Three days is also the point at which a cream starts to matter. Most ordinary irritant rashes clear without any cream. The exception is a rash around the anus during diarrhea, which does better under a protective ointment such as Vaseline or Desitin. A yeast rash needs an anti-yeast cream. Lotrimin AF is one, and it is sold without a prescription. Call us right away if the skin is bright red and peeling off in sheets, or if your baby has a fever and the redness is spreading. A baby under a month old also needs to be seen right away for grouped water blisters or pimples, for yellow scabs, or for looking or acting abnormal in any way. The When To Call tab below covers everything else, including open sores and a rash that spreads beyond the diaper area.

### head-injury
Paragraph 1: Watch how your child wakes, talks, walks and keeps food down. Call 911 if your child had a seizure, was knocked out for more than a minute, or is hard to wake. Call 911 as well for confused talk, slurred speech, unsteady walking, or weakness in an arm or leg that your child has right now. Major bleeding you cannot stop is a 911 call too. If your child is not moving the neck normally, call 911 and keep the neck still.
Paragraph 2: Those same four signs still matter after they pass. If your child had confused talk, slurred speech, unsteady walking or weakness in an arm or leg after the injury and seems fine now, go straight to an emergency room. A child who is awake but not alert, unfocused or slow to respond may have a mild concussion, and that also means an emergency room. Go for neck pain after the injury, for vomiting two or more times, or for a severe headache or crying that will not stop. Go too if your child cannot remember what happened or cannot store new memories, which a parent notices as the same question asked over and over. Blurred or double vision lasting more than five minutes also means an emergency room. So does an injury at high speed, such as a car crash, and so does a cut deep enough to need many stitches.
Paragraph 3: A big soft lump on the scalp looks frightening and usually means very little. The scalp has a large blood supply and bleeds easily into the loose tissue beneath, so a small injury can raise a large swelling. The skull bone separates that swelling from the brain. Size still counts, and swelling wider than one inch means calling your office's main number now, at any hour. Feel the skull under the swelling as well, because a dent in the bone means calling that same number now, whatever the lump looks like. Your child can sleep. Wake your child once, two hours after they fall asleep, and check that they know you and can talk and walk normally. The When To Call tab below covers the rest.

### suicide-concerns
Lead: Call 911 now if your child has attempted suicide in the past 24 hours, or is confused and not making sense, which can happen after taking something. Go to an emergency room tonight if your child has threatened suicide or has a plan. If you are frightened and none of that has happened yet, call or text 988, the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline, at any hour.
Paragraph 1: A plan means your child has a method in mind, such as taking drugs or using a weapon. Go to an emergency room as well if your child attempted suicide at any point in the past week. Go tonight too if you think your child has been using drugs or alcohol and is showing the effects right now, or if your child has needed a psychiatric hospital before for symptoms like these. Do not leave your child alone. Call us as well, at any hour, and we will talk with you about what comes next. Our behavioral health services are for children who are already patients at Wasatch Pediatrics.
Paragraph 2: Making the house safer is the next thing to do, and firearms come first, because firearms cause most suicide deaths in North America. Move any firearms out of the house to a relative or a friend for now. If a firearm stays in the house, unload it and lock it away. Then go through whatever else your child could reach, starting with medicines and alcohol. The Care Advice tab below has the rest of the home safety plan.
Paragraph 3: Ask your child directly whether they are thinking of killing themselves. Plain words are safer than careful ones, and asking the question does not put the idea in your child's head. Listen without arguing. Do not promise to keep what your child tells you secret, and say that you are getting help because you love them. Thoughts of suicide, talk of having no reason to live, and themes of death in what your child draws or writes all need attention now. Call or text 988 at any hour.

### constipation
Paragraph 1: Holding back works for an evening and fails over a week. Your child is constipated if passing a stool hurts or brings on crying, if straining for more than ten minutes produces nothing, or if three or more days pass with no stool. A child who goes four or five days almost always has pain and hard straining when the stool finally comes, and that pain is the reason to hold on again. Pain is one trigger. A power struggle is the other, and it is the most common cause of repeated constipation. The struggle usually starts over toilet training, and in a child who is already trained it often begins with the start of school, because some children refuse to use public toilets. Pressure and punishment make the holding back worse.
Paragraph 2: Go straight to an emergency room if your child's vomit is green, meaning the green of bile rather than the yellow of stomach juice. Other signs mean calling us the same day. Belly or rectal pain lasting more than an hour, vomiting twice or more with a swollen belly, and a new weak suck in a baby under a year all count. The When To Call tab below has the rest. Otherwise constipation starts at home, with changes to food and a calmer routine, and the Care Advice tab below covers them. Call us before you start a stool softener, so that we can go over which one suits your child and how much to give. A breastfed baby over a month old can go four to seven days between stools without being constipated, as long as those stools stay soft and painless. Under a month old, call us instead, because a breastfed newborn who rarely stools may not be getting enough milk.
