# Round 3 blind evaluation B (reader) — FAIL

## The finding that matters: rule 17 was applied too far

Three places where the page raises a threshold and then withholds it. Two are
promised in the page's own meta description.

- **fever.** "I came with a number in my hand, 103.2, and I wanted to know if
  that number is dangerous... it never tells me what temperature counts as a
  fever at all. The meta description literally promises 'what counts as a fever
  in a young baby' and the page doesn't say it. I finished not knowing whether
  100.1 in my 10-week-old is a fever I should be calling about at midnight."
- **head-injury.** "One is how wide the swelling has spread, and the When To Call
  tab below gives the width that matters." Reader: "You've just told me a width
  matters and then refused to tell me the width. I'm looking at the lump right
  now with my hand on it."
- **constipation.** "The description promises 'what actually counts as
  constipated' and the page never says it... my only action was don't punish him
  and call before you buy anything, which doesn't get me through tonight."
- **croup.** "The meta description promised me 'what to do in the next twenty
  minutes.' Then the page gives me twenty minutes of nothing to do and points at
  a tab. One line, cool night air or steamy bathroom, whichever it is, would have
  been the most useful sentence on the site."

## High severity, other

- **fever, contradiction.** Paragraph 1: "Do they play a little once fever
  medicine has taken the edge off?" Paragraph 2: "Hold the fever medicine until
  we have seen your baby." Reader had to scroll back and work out that the second
  applies only under 12 weeks. "At 2am with a hot baby I might well have taken
  the wrong instruction."
- **croup, blue lips.** "Lips or a face turning bluish when your child is not
  coughing mean 911 as well." Reader: "So if he goes blue while coughing that's
  fine? I reread this three times."
- **head-injury, paragraph 1 unreadable.** A dozen emergencies in one unbroken
  block. "I read the first three sentences and gave up on the rest."
- **head-injury, the two lists.** "So does confused talk, slurred speech,
  unsteady walking or weakness that has now passed off." The same list appeared
  two sentences earlier as a 911 call. The distinction is right now versus
  already stopped, and it is buried. "Passed off" was first read as "passed out",
  the opposite meaning.
- **head-injury, the dent.** "The other is a dent you can feel in the skull." No
  verb follows. "A dent in my child's skull seems like it deserves a verb."
- **head-injury, two-hour clocks, still.** "Separately" reads as patching over a
  contradiction rather than explaining a sequence.
- **suicide-concerns, ordering.** "Then go through the house" arrives directly
  after go to an emergency room and do not leave your child alone.
- **suicide-concerns, symptoms of what.** "if you suspect drug or alcohol use and
  your child has symptoms now" — symptoms of being high, of withdrawal, of
  suicidality.
- **constipation, whiplash.** "Go straight to an emergency room if your child's
  vomit is green. Changes to food and a calmer routine come first at home."
  Emergency room, then dinner. Also: how green counts as green.

## Medium and low

- fever: "unremarkable" is a doctor's word; "gone too weak to stand or move"
  does not fit a baby who cannot stand anyway; "under 12 weeks" forces arithmetic
  on a parent who thinks in months; "harder for us to read" says nothing.
- fever: four consecutive sentences opening "Go".
- croup: the first body sentence repeats the lead almost word for word; "Never
  dismiss stridor because your child happened to be crying" repeats the sentence
  before it.
- diaper-rash: "Its redness runs deeper" — darker, or deeper into the skin.
- diaper-rash: "not only in newborns" answers an objection the page never raised.
- diaper-rash: whether the yeast cream needs a prescription is the practical
  question at 11pm and it is behind the tab.
- suicide-concerns: "988, 911 and the emergency room are open to every family"
  reads as the practice covering itself, and plants a doubt the reader did not
  have.
- suicide-concerns: "Firearms cause most suicide deaths in North America" reads
  as copied from a report.
- suicide-concerns: no answer to what happens tomorrow, or whether the
  pediatrician can help beyond tonight.
- constipation: "meets pain and hard straining"; "the exception to all of this"
  has no boundary; paragraph 2 holds five unrelated things.

## What worked

- suicide-concerns: "Ask your child directly... asking the question does not put
  the idea in your child's head." Named the best writing across all six pages.
- head-injury: "The skull bone separates that swelling from the brain."
- head-injury: "Your child can sleep."
- diaper-rash: "Two different rashes wear the same name." The only lead of the
  six understood on the first pass without effort.
- constipation: "Call us before you start a stool softener, so that we can go
  over which one suits your child and how much to give."
