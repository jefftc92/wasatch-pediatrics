# Symptom pages for evaluation

## What each page is for

Each page sits above a live panel from the American Academy of Pediatrics
carrying three tabs on the same topic: Definition, When To Call, and Care
Advice. The AAP writes and updates that panel.

The division of labour is fixed by rule 17 and it is the point of this round.
Our prose carries the Call 911 Now and Go to ER Now signs in full, plus the
judgement behind a decision and what this practice does. Every lower AAP tier
and all home treatment belong to the panel, and our prose names the tab rather
than repeating it. A page that sends the reader to a named tab for lower-tier
guidance is following the rule, not omitting something.

## Who reads it

A parent with a sick or worried child. On a phone. Often at night. No medical
training, no patience, looking for one specific answer.

## Factual constraints

- Ages, temperatures, durations and thresholds must match the AAP source,
  cached at /tmp/aap/<slug>.html.
- Where our page and the AAP disagree about urgency, the AAP wins. We may be
  more cautious than the source. Never less.
- These claims are published on the site and are supported. Do not flag them:
  a 24/7 line reached on each office's own main number, on which "you will
  reach a nurse or a physician, not an answering service"; same-day sick
  visits at every office; After Hours Care at most offices, by appointment,
  no walk-ins; "behavioral health services are available at our Wasatch
  Pediatric offices", and those services "are for established patients only".
- Anything else asserted about what the practice does must be traceable to a
  published page.

## The text under review

### fever

H1: Fever in Children
Meta description: Most childhood fevers come from ordinary viruses. What counts as a fever, which fevers mean 911 or an emergency room, and why any fever under 3 months means calling us tonight.
Heading: Judging a fever by your child, not the thermometer

Lead: Most childhood fevers come from ordinary viruses, and how your child drinks, responds and plays tells you more about this one than the number does.

Paragraph 1: Three questions settle most fevers at home. Is your child drinking? Do they respond to you normally? Do they play a little once fever medicine has taken the edge off? A child who does all three is usually well enough to watch at home, whatever the number says. A true fever means a temperature of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher taken rectally, in the ear or on the forehead, 100°F (37.8°C) by mouth, or 99°F (37.2°C) under the arm. Take the temperature rectally in a baby under 6 months, because ear thermometers are not accurate before that age. Call 911 for a child who cannot be woken, or who has stopped moving or is too weak to stand, even when the temperature itself looks ordinary.

Paragraph 2: Call 911 as well if your child is struggling for each breath and can barely speak or cry, or if purple or blood-colored spots appear on the skin. An emergency room is the right place for a stiff neck or for a seizure during the fever. Go as well if your baby is under a year old and the soft spot on the head bulges or swells. Take your child there too if they are hard to wake, are not alert when awake, or act or talk confused. Any fever in a baby under 3 months old, which is 12 weeks, means calling us at any hour, because at that age we check rather than wait. Do not give a baby under 3 months any fever medicine before we have seen them. The When To Call tab below lists the fevers that need a call within a day, and the Care Advice tab covers comfort and dosing.

### croup

H1: Croup in Children
Meta description: Croup's barking cough and noisy breathing: what stridor sounds like, what to do in the next twenty minutes, and which signs mean 911 or an emergency room.
Heading: Listen to the breathing in, not the cough

Lead: Croup makes a barking, seal-like cough that gets worse at night. The cough is not what decides how urgent croup is. Listen instead for stridor, a harsh, raspy sound your child makes when breathing in.

Paragraph 1: Crying makes stridor louder, so comfort your child first and listen again once they have settled. Stridor you can still hear while your child is calm and resting means severe croup, and so does stridor that is loud or constant. Warm mist is the first thing to try. Run a hot shower in a closed bathroom and sit with your child in the mist for 20 minutes, away from the water itself. If the warm mist does not help, cold air sometimes does, and the Care Advice tab below has that step and the rest of the home care.

Paragraph 2: Call 911 if your child is struggling for each breath, if the stridor is constant and severe, or if your child passes out or stops breathing. Bluish lips or a bluish face while your child is not coughing means 911, and so do drooling, spitting or great trouble swallowing. Croup that began suddenly after a bee sting, a new medicine, or a food your child is allergic to is also a 911 call. Go straight to an emergency room if your baby is under a year old and has stridor. Go as well if the ribs pull in with each breath, or if your child may have choked on a small object. Lips that turn blue during a coughing fit are not safe either, and neither is stridor you can hear right now in a child over a year old. Call your office's main number at any hour for either sign, because a child with stridor needs to be seen rather than advised over the phone.

### diaper-rash

H1: Diaper Rash
Meta description: Diaper rash in babies: how to tell an ordinary rash from a yeast infection, which rashes need to be seen right away, and when to call us.
Heading: Ordinary diaper rash, and the yeast kind

Lead: Two different rashes wear the same name, and they need different treatment.

Paragraph 1: Ordinary diaper rash comes from stool sitting against the skin, so wetness on its own is rarely the problem. Getting the stool off the skin at every change is most of the treatment, and the Care Advice tab below gives the method. A yeast rash looks different. Its red is bright, and its border is a sharp line rather than a fade. The skin inside that border can look raw and weepy, and separate red spots or small pimples sit just beyond the border.

Paragraph 2: Time tells you which rash your baby has. A rash that is no better after three days of warm-water cleaning and air exposure has probably picked up yeast, because yeast does not clear with cleaning alone. Three days is also the point at which a cream starts to matter. An ordinary irritant rash needs no cream at all, and a yeast rash needs an anti-yeast cream you can buy over the counter without a prescription. Call us right away if the skin is bright red and peeling off in sheets, or if your baby has a fever and the redness is spreading. A baby under a month old also needs to be seen right away for grouped water blisters or pimples, for yellow scabs, or for looking or acting abnormal in any way. The When To Call tab below covers everything else, including open sores and a rash that spreads beyond the diaper area.

### head-injury

H1: Head Injury in Children
Meta description: Head injuries in children: which signs mean 911 or an emergency room, whether your child can sleep afterward, and when to call us.
Heading: The lump matters less than how your child acts

Lead: Most head injuries damage only the scalp, and what your child does over the next few hours shows whether this one did more.

Paragraph 1: Watch how your child wakes, talks, walks and keeps food down. Call 911 if your child had a seizure, was knocked out for more than a minute, or is hard to wake. Four more signs mean 911 when your child has them right now: confused talk, slurred speech, unsteady walking, or weakness in an arm or leg. Major bleeding you cannot stop is a 911 call too. If your child is not moving the neck normally, call 911 and keep the neck still.

Paragraph 2: Go straight to an emergency room if your child had confused talk, slurred speech, unsteady walking or weakness in an arm or leg after the injury and is fine now. A child who is awake but not alert, unfocused or slow to respond may have a mild concussion, and that also means an emergency room. Go for neck pain after the injury, for vomiting two or more times, or for a severe headache or crying that will not stop. Go too if your child cannot remember what happened or cannot store new memories, which a parent notices as the same question asked over and over. Blurred or double vision lasting more than five minutes also means an emergency room. So does an injury at high speed, such as a car crash, and so does a cut deep enough to need many stitches.

Paragraph 3: A big soft lump on the scalp looks frightening and usually means very little. The scalp has a large blood supply and bleeds easily into the loose tissue beneath, so a small injury can raise a large swelling. The skull bone separates that swelling from the brain. Two things about a lump do change what we do. Swelling wider than one inch, about 2.5 centimeters, means calling your office's main number now, at any hour. So does a dent you can feel in the skull. Your child can sleep. Wake your child once, two hours after they fall asleep, and check that they know you and can talk and walk normally. The When To Call tab below covers the rest.

### suicide-concerns

H1: Suicide Concerns
Meta description: If you are worried your child may be suicidal: 988, when to call 911 or go to an emergency room, making the home safer, and what to say.
Heading: Getting help tonight, and making the house safer

Lead: Call 911 now if your child attempted suicide in the past 24 hours, or is acting or talking confused. If you are worried your child may be suicidal and there has been no attempt, call or text 988. The Suicide and Crisis Lifeline answers 24 hours a day.

Paragraph 1: Go to an emergency room if your child attempted suicide in the past week, has threatened suicide, or has a plan such as taking drugs or using a weapon. Go as well if you think your child has been using drugs or alcohol and is showing the effects right now. Go too if your child has needed a psychiatric hospital before for symptoms like these. Do not leave your child alone. If your child is an established patient at Wasatch Pediatrics, call us as well and we will talk with you about what comes next.

Paragraph 2: Making the house safer is the next thing to do, and firearms come first. Move any firearms out of the house to a relative or a friend for now. If a firearm stays in the house, unload it and lock it away. Firearms cause most suicide deaths in North America, which is why they come before everything else on this list. Lock up medicines and alcohol too, and for an older teen, consider holding on to the car keys.

Paragraph 3: Ask your child directly whether they are thinking of killing themselves. Plain words are safer than careful ones, and asking the question does not put the idea in your child's head. Listen without arguing. Do not promise to keep what your child tells you secret, and say that you are getting help because you love them. Thoughts of suicide, talk of having no reason to live, and themes of death in what your child draws or writes all need attention now. Call or text 988 at any hour.

### constipation

H1: Constipation in Children
Meta description: Constipation in children: what actually counts as constipated, why a child starts holding back, and when to call us.
Heading: Why children hold stools back, and why that keeps constipation going

Lead: A child who has had one painful stool will often hold the next one back, and holding back is what turns a bad week into a pattern.

Paragraph 1: Holding back works for an evening and fails over a week. Your child is constipated if passing a stool hurts or brings on crying, if straining for more than ten minutes produces nothing, or if three or more days pass with no stool. A child who goes four or five days almost always has pain and hard straining when the stool finally comes, and that pain is the reason to hold on again. Pain is one trigger. A power struggle is the other, and it is the most common cause of repeated constipation. The struggle usually starts over toilet training, and in a child who is already trained it often begins with the start of school, because some children refuse to use public toilets. Pressure and punishment make the holding back worse, so a child who is being pushed holds on harder.

Paragraph 2: Go straight to an emergency room if your child's vomit is green, meaning the green of bile rather than the yellow of stomach juice. Green vomit is the rare exception. Everything else starts at home, with changes to food and a calmer routine, and the Care Advice tab below has them. Call us before you start a stool softener, so that we can go over which one suits your child and how much to give. A breastfed baby over a month old can go four to seven days between stools without being constipated, as long as those stools stay soft and painless. That exception is about how often a stool comes and nothing else. The When To Call tab below covers belly pain, bleeding and constipation that will not shift.
