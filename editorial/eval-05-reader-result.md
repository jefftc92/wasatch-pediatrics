# Round 5 blind evaluation B (reader) — FAIL

Three pages passed cleanly: diaper-rash, head-injury, suicide-concerns. The lead
on suicide-concerns was named the best writing in the set. Constipation passed
"but only after reading the whole page, and it scared me first."

## The three sendbacks

### 1. croup, stridor at rest, two answers for one description

Paragraph 1: "Stridor you can still hear while your child is calm and resting
means severe croup," and severe croup "needs to be seen tonight."
Paragraph 2: "Call 911 if... the stridor is constant and severe."

Reader: "My child is sitting still and I can hear the noise every time she
breathes in... Those sound like the same thing described twice with different
answers. I read both twice and still don't know whether to dial 911 or get my
coat."

**This is rule 19.** A parent cannot separate "constant and severe" from "at
rest" while it is happening, and guessing wrong routes them down a tier.
Collapse upward: stridor you can hear while the child is calm means 911, with
warm mist while help is on the way.

### 2. fever, "cannot be woken" against "hard to wake"

911 for a child who "cannot be woken", emergency room if they are "hard to
wake", in different paragraphs.

Reader: "My child is groggy and floppy and takes a lot of shaking. Is that
cannot be woken or hard to wake? I'm being asked to grade my own child on a
distinction I can't make."

**Also rule 19.** The AAP does split these, and the reader cannot. Collapse
upward.

### 3. constipation, the breastfed exception arrives last

Reader with a breastfed two-month-old who had not gone in four days: "I read the
first line, decided she was constipated and got worried, then read on through
emergency-room signs, green vomit and stool softeners before finding out at the
very bottom that four to seven days is normal for her. The reassurance I needed
was the last thing on the page, behind a green-bile warning that frightened me
on the way past."

Move the exception next to the definition it excepts.

## Other findings

- **fever.** "even at 103" then "Above 104°F, call us the same day" leaves 104.0
  undefined. "My thermometer says 104.0. Is that above 104?" Say 104°F or higher.
- **fever.** The definition of a fever arrives mid-paragraph, after the advice
  about 103 and 104. "I read past it once because I wasn't expecting the basic
  definition to arrive that late."
- **croup.** "Call us either way" — either way of what. Mist working or not,
  cold air, or the age split. Three candidate pairs.
- **croup.** "needs to be seen rather than advised over the phone" is an odd
  argument for making a phone call.
- **croup.** Three separate instructions to call, so the reader was skimming by
  the third, which is the one carrying the age split.
- **head-injury.** "Those same four signs still matter after they pass." The
  reader scrolled up and counted them, then the next sentence lists all four
  anyway. Cutting the sentence costs nothing.
- **head-injury.** "that same number" sends the reader looking back.
- **suicide-concerns.** "Our behavioral health services are for children who are
  already patients at Wasatch Pediatrics." "In the middle of the worst night of
  my life I hit a membership rule... it lands like a door closing at the exact
  point I was being told help exists."
- **suicide-concerns.** "in North America" reads as quoting a study.
- **diaper-rash.** "a cream starts to matter" is ambiguous between start a cream
  and a cream becomes diagnostically relevant, and the next sentence says creams

- **constipation.** "Holding back works for an evening and fails over a week"
  needed unpacking, and the lead above it already said the same thing plainly.
