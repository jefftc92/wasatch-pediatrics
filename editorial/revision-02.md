### fever
Heading: Judging a fever by your child, not the thermometer
Meta description: Most childhood fevers come from ordinary viruses. Which fevers need care right away, which can wait a day, and what counts as a fever in a young baby.
Lead: Most childhood fevers come from ordinary viruses, and how your child drinks, responds and plays tells you more about this one than the number does.
Paragraph 1: Three questions settle most fevers at home. Is your child drinking? Do they respond to you normally? Do they play a little once fever medicine has taken the edge off? A child who does all three is usually well enough to watch at home, whatever the thermometer says. A child who has gone limp, cannot be woken, or has stopped drinking needs to be seen, even when the reading looks unremarkable.
Paragraph 2: Some fevers are not a judgment call. Call 911 if your child cannot be woken, is too weak to stand or move, or is struggling for each breath. Call 911 as well for purple or blood-colored spots on the skin. Go straight to an emergency room for a stiff neck, a seizure with the fever, or a child who is hard to wake or acting confused. Go too for great trouble swallowing fluids or spit. Three fevers need care right away. The first is any fever in a baby under 12 weeks old, meaning a rectal temperature of 100.4°F or higher. Give that baby no fever medicine before the visit. The second is a fever above 104°F. The third is a fever with signs of dehydration: no urine for more than eight hours, dark urine, a very dry mouth and no tears. Call us within a day for any fever in a baby three to six months old, for a fever lasting more than three days, and for a fever that comes back after being gone more than 24 hours. Between six and 24 months, call within a day if a fever with no other symptom lasts more than 48 hours.

### croup
Heading: Listen to the breathing in, not the cough
Meta description: Croup's barking cough and noisy breathing: what stridor sounds like, what to do in the next twenty minutes, and which signs mean 911 or an emergency room.
Lead: Croup makes a barking, seal-like cough that gets worse at night. What decides how urgent it is comes not from the cough but from a harsh, raspy sound when your child breathes in, called stridor.
Paragraph 1: Stridor is the sign that matters, and you hear it on the way in rather than on the cough. Loud or constant stridor means severe croup, and so does stridor while your child is calm and at rest. Crying makes stridor worse, so comfort your child first and listen again once they have settled. Do not dismiss stridor because your child was crying when you first heard it. While you listen, run a hot shower in a closed bathroom and sit with your child in the warm mist for 20 minutes. Do not use very hot water or steam, which can burn. If the mist does not help and the night is cold, take your child outside for a few minutes. Croup usually runs five to six days.
Paragraph 2: Call 911 if your child is struggling for each breath, passes out or stops breathing, or turns bluish in the lips or face when not coughing. Drooling, spitting or great trouble swallowing needs 911 too, unless the drooling is from teething. So does croup that began suddenly after a bee sting, a new medicine, or a food your child is allergic to. Go straight to an emergency room if your baby is under a year old and has stridor. Go as well if the ribs pull in with each breath, or if your child may have choked on a small object. For stridor in an older child, call your office's main number at any hour. Most children with stridor need a steroid such as Decadron, so expect a visit rather than a phone call alone.

### diaper-rash
Heading: Ordinary diaper rash, and the yeast kind
Meta description: Diaper rash in babies: how to tell an ordinary rash from a yeast infection, which rashes need to be seen right away, and when to call us.
Lead: Two different rashes wear the same name, and they need different treatment.
Paragraph 1: Ordinary diaper rash comes from stool sitting against the skin, so wetness on its own is rarely the problem. Rinse the stool off with plenty of warm water at every change and leave the bottom open to air. Most rashes clear in about three days of that. A yeast rash behaves differently. The redness is deeper and the border is a hard line rather than a fade. The skin inside it can look raw and wet, and separate red spots or small pimples sit just outside the border.
Paragraph 2: A rash still there after three days of that care has probably picked up yeast. An anti-yeast cream such as Lotrimin AF treats it, no prescription needed, twice a day. Call us right away if your baby has a fever and the rash looks infected with redness spreading out from it, or if bright red skin is peeling off in sheets. In a baby under one month old, call right away for tiny water blisters or pimples in a group, for yellow scabs or spreading redness, or for a baby who looks or acts abnormal in any way. At any age, call us within a day about pimples, blisters, boils or open sores. Tell us within a day too if the rash is no better after three days of yeast cream.

### head-injury
Heading: How your child acts in the next few hours, and when the lump itself matters
Meta description: Head injuries in children: which signs mean 911 or an emergency room, whether your child can sleep afterward, and when to call us.
Lead: Most head injuries damage only the scalp, and what your child does over the next few hours shows whether this one did more.
Paragraph 1: Watch how your child wakes, talks, walks and keeps food down. Call 911 if your child had a seizure, was knocked out for more than a minute, or is hard to wake. Call 911 too if your child acts or talks confused, or the speech is slurred now. The same goes for unsteady walking or weakness in an arm or leg. If your child is not moving the neck normally, call 911 and keep the neck still. Go straight to an emergency room for neck pain after the injury. Go as well if your child has vomited two or more times, cannot remember what happened, or has a severe headache or crying that will not stop.
Paragraph 2: A big soft lump on the scalp looks frightening and usually means very little. The scalp has a large blood supply and bleeds easily into the loose tissue beneath, so a small injury can raise a large swelling. The skull bone separates that swelling from the brain. Two things about a lump change the answer: swelling wider than an inch, and a dent in the skull. Call us right away about either. Call right away too after a blow from a hard object such as a golf club, or a fall from a dangerous height. A baby under a year old needs a call right away after any head injury, and so does a child who was knocked out even briefly. Your child can sleep. Watch closely for the first two hours after the injury, then wake your child after two hours of sleep and check that they know you and can talk and walk normally. Sleep in the same room the first night, and call us during office hours if a headache lasts more than 24 hours.

### suicide-concerns
Heading: Say the words, and remove the means
Meta description: If you are worried your child may be suicidal: 988, when to call 911 or go to an emergency room, making the home safer, and what to say.
Lead: Call or text 988 now if you think your child may be suicidal, whether they said something, wrote something, or you simply cannot shake the feeling. The Suicide and Crisis Lifeline answers 24 hours a day.
Paragraph 1: Call 911 if your child has attempted suicide in the past 24 hours, or is confused in speech or behavior. Go to an emergency room if your child has threatened suicide, has a plan, or has attempted suicide within the past week. Do not leave your child alone. Then go through the house. Lock away any firearms and make sure they are unloaded, and better still, store them with a relative or friend for now. Firearms cause most suicide deaths in North America, which is why they come first. Lock up medicines and alcohol. For an older teen, consider holding on to the car keys.
Paragraph 2: Ask your child directly whether they are thinking of killing themselves. Plain words are safer than careful ones, and asking the question does not put the idea in your child's head. Listen without arguing. Do not promise to keep what your child tells you secret, and say that you are getting help because you love them. Thoughts of suicide, talk of having no reason to live, and themes of death in what your child draws or writes all need attention now. Call 988 at any hour. 988, 911 and the emergency room are open to every family, whether or not your child is our patient. Behavioral health services at our offices are for established patients only, so if your child is one, call us as well and we will talk about what comes next.

### constipation
Heading: Why children hold stools back, and why that keeps constipation going
Meta description: Constipation in children: what actually counts as constipated, why a child starts holding back, and when to call us.
Lead: A child who has had one painful stool will often hold the next one back, and holding back is what turns a bad week into a pattern.
Paragraph 1: Constipation means pain or crying when passing a stool, straining for more than ten minutes without success, or three or more days without a stool. Breastfed babies over a month old are the exception, and can go longer as long as the stools stay soft and painless. Holding back is what keeps constipation going. A child who goes four or five days almost always has pain and hard straining when the stool finally comes, and that pain buys another day of holding. Pain is one trigger. The other, and the most common cause of repeated constipation, is a power struggle, usually over toilet training or a refusal to use school toilets. Pressure and punishment make holding back worse. Praise, treats and small rewards are what get a child to give it up.
Paragraph 2: Changes to food and a calmer routine come first. When they are not enough, a stool softener is the next step, and we would rather go over it with you before you start. Miralax is sold without a prescription and is for children over one year old. The usual amount is one to three teaspoons of powder a day with dinner, each teaspoon mixed into two ounces of water, and it should produce regular soft stools within one to three days. Vomiting is the one thing here that can mean an emergency. Go straight to an emergency room if your child's vomit is green. Call us the same day if your child vomits twice or more and the belly looks more swollen than usual. Call the same day too if belly or rectal pain lasts more than an hour, if there is bleeding from the anus, or if your child looks or acts very sick. Call during office hours if constipation lasts more than a week after you have changed what your child eats, or if painful stools keep happening.
