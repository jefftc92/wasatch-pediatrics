# Draft 08 — structure check (pre-apply)

Run by editorial-structure on the draft, before it was applied. This is the
check that did not exist through rounds 2 to 7.

## The regression it caught

**fever now runs call-now, then 911, then emergency room.** Rule 20 does not
only forbid splitting a run, it fixes the order: 911 first, then ER, then
everything else. The draft puts the page's lowest routing tier first and its
highest on screen three.

> "A parent who scans two paragraphs of 'call us now, at any hour' reasonably
> reads that as the page's ceiling and stops. What they miss: all four 911
> signs, including purple or blood-colored spots."

That is the same sign the round 6 reader nearly missed, about to be missed again
for a new reason. Fever would also have been the only page in the batch carrying
911 items that does not put them in the first paragraph or the lead.

This is standing question 9 exactly: the rewriter was fixing a real round 7
finding, that the under-3-months rule sat behind ten emergency signs, and fixed
it by moving the whole call-now block in front of the whole 911 block.

The checker proposed a reshuffle that keeps both fixes: the under-3-months rule
is not a routing tier but the sentence that says "for this baby, do not judge,
call", so it belongs with the judging material in paragraph 1. Then 911 and ER
in paragraph 2, and the 104 and 106 thresholds in paragraph 3.

## Other findings

1. **The urgency token is at the wrong end of the two lines previously missed.**
   Fever runs three sentences opening "Call 911 if", then "Purple or
   blood-colored spots or dots on the skin mean 911 as well." A parent scanning
   the left edge gets three hits and slides past the fourth. Croup has the same
   shape on blue lips, on drooling, and on the bee-sting line, which carries no
   urgency token at all and leans on "So does" reaching back three sentences.
   Blue lips and drooling are precisely the pair the round 6 reader reported.
2. **head-injury compressed too far.** The four signs and a two-branch timing
   decision now sit in one 33-word sentence. Rule 20 says name the signs once
   **then** split by timing. The two-sentence version was easier to hold, and the
   duplication fault does not return because the second sentence says "those
   signs" rather than reprinting them.
3. **head-injury paragraph 2 opens "An emergency room is also right"** where four
   sibling pages state precedence explicitly with "If none of those signs fit".
4. **croup and constipation each have a routing sentence with three coordinators
   and mixed and/or.** The parent evaluates a compound condition against a
   compound exclusion.
5. **"A child who does all three"** is the counting-out-loud shape again, making
   the reader scroll back to check they answered three.
6. **croup's lead fails rule 4.** The heading says listen to the breathing in,
   not the cough, and sentence one describes the cough. The answer arrives in
   sentence three. Outside the draft's diff, so a decision for the rewriter.
7. **diaper-rash restates two identifying signs verbatim across the paragraph
   boundary.** A hinge rather than a reprinted list, but it is the smaller cousin
   of the head-injury fault that got reported twice.

## What it passed

- No 911 or ER run is split by a paragraph break on any of the six pages. The
  round 6 fault has not recurred in its original form.
- No list of signs is printed twice. The head-injury duplication reported in two
  consecutive rounds is gone.
- suicide-concerns is "the best-shaped page in the batch": 911, then the ER run
  unmixed with its exclusion stated in words, then 988 behind an explicit
  precedence opener. The two ER items stranded in paragraph 1 in round 7 are now
  consolidated into the lead.
- croup's three-paragraph split works, and paragraph 3 opening "start it while
  you wait for help" is the right cross-reference for a parent who has called.
- Rule 4 passes on five of six pages. Only one instance of counting out loud
  survives, and it is a carryover rather than new.
