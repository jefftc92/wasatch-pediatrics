# Revision 07 — six symptom pages

### fever
Heading: Judging a fever by your child, not the thermometer
Meta description: What counts as a fever, how to judge your child rather than the number, which fevers mean 911 or an emergency room, and why any fever in a baby under 3 months means calling us at any hour.
Lead: Most childhood fevers come from ordinary viruses, and how your child behaves tells you more about this one than the thermometer does.
Paragraph 1: Start with your child rather than the reading. Is your child drinking? Do they respond to you normally? Do they play a little once the fever eases, either on its own or after a dose of fever medicine? A child who does all three is usually well enough to watch at home. A true fever means 100.4°F or higher taken rectally, in the ear or on the forehead, 100°F by mouth, or 99°F under the arm. Any of those readings counts, though an ear thermometer is not reliable under 6 months, and forehead strips are not reliable at any age. A high number is uncomfortable rather than dangerous, and a reading of 103°F in a child who is drinking and playing is still a fever to watch at home. At 104°F or higher, call us now, at any hour, even if your child seems well. The heat itself is still not damaging your child. We ask for the call because we would rather hear about a fever that high while it is happening than after it has passed.
Paragraph 2: Call 911 if your child is not moving or is too weak to stand, even when the temperature itself looks ordinary. Call 911 if your child is hard to wake or cannot be woken at all. Call 911 if your child is struggling for each breath and can barely speak or cry. Purple or blood-colored spots or dots on the skin mean 911 as well. If none of those signs fit, an emergency room is the right place for a stiff neck or for a seizure during the fever. Take your child to an emergency room if they are awake but not alert, or act or talk confused. Take a baby under a year old to an emergency room if the soft spot on the head bulges or swells. Any fever in a baby under 3 months old, which is 12 weeks, means calling us now, at any hour, because at that age we check rather than wait. Do not give a baby under 3 months any fever medicine before we have seen them. The When To Call tab below lists the fevers that need a call within a day, and the Care Advice tab covers comfort and dosing.

### croup
Paragraph 1: Stridor is the sound to listen for, and crying makes it louder, so comfort your child first and listen again once they have settled. If you can still hear stridor while your child is calm and resting, call 911, whatever your child's age. Call 911 if your child is struggling for each breath, or if your child passes out or stops breathing. Lips or a face turning blue mean 911, whether or not your child is coughing at the time. Drooling, spitting or great trouble swallowing means 911. So does croup that started suddenly after a bee sting, a new medicine, or a food your child is allergic to.
Paragraph 2: If none of those signs fit, go straight to an emergency room when the ribs pull in with each breath, or when your child may have choked on a small object. A baby under a year old needs an emergency room now for stridor that comes only with crying or being upset. For a child over a year old whose stridor comes only with crying, call your office's main number now, at any hour. Warm mist helps croup at every level, so start it while you wait or while you decide. Run a hot shower in a closed bathroom and sit with your child in the mist for twenty minutes, staying away from the water so that nobody gets burned. The Care Advice tab below has the rest of the home care.

### diaper-rash
Meta description: Diaper rash in babies: how to tell an ordinary rash from a yeast infection, what to put on each one, and which rashes need us to see your baby now.
Paragraph 1: Ordinary diaper rash comes from stool sitting against the skin, so wetness on its own is rarely the problem. Getting the stool off the skin at every change is most of the treatment, and the Care Advice tab below gives the method. Cleaning alone clears most ordinary rashes, with one exception. A rash around the anus while your baby has diarrhea does better under a protective ointment such as Vaseline or Desitin, which keeps stool off the skin. A yeast rash looks different. Its red is bright, and its border is a sharp line rather than a fade. The skin inside that border can look raw and weepy, and separate red spots or small pimples sit just beyond the border.
Paragraph 2: The look of the rash tells you which one to suspect, and time confirms the answer. A rash that is no better after three days of cleaning with warm water and leaving the skin open to the air has probably picked up yeast. Yeast does not clear with cleaning, so three days without improvement is the point to start an anti-yeast cream. Lotrimin AF is one, and it is sold without a prescription. Call us now, at any hour, if the skin is bright red and peeling off in sheets, or if your baby has a fever and the redness is spreading. A baby under a month old also needs to be seen now for grouped water blisters or pimples, for yellow scabs, or for looking or acting abnormal in any way. The When To Call tab below covers everything else, including open sores and a rash that spreads beyond the diaper area.

### head-injury
Lead: Most head injuries damage only the scalp, and what your child does over the next few hours shows whether this one did more. Your child can sleep. Wake them once, two hours after they fall asleep, and check that they know you and can talk and walk normally.
Paragraph 1: Watch how your child wakes, talks, walks and keeps food down. Call 911 if your child had a seizure, was knocked out even briefly, or is hard to wake. Major bleeding you cannot stop is a 911 call too. If your child is not moving the neck normally, call 911 and keep the neck still. Confused talk, slurred speech, unsteady walking and weakness in an arm or leg mean 911 while your child has them. If any of those signs came after the injury and have already passed, drive to an emergency room instead.
Paragraph 2: An emergency room is also right for a child who is awake but not alert, unfocused or slow to respond, because those are the signs of a concussion. Go for neck pain after the injury, for vomiting two or more times, or for a severe headache or crying that will not stop. Go if your child cannot remember what happened or cannot store new memories, which a parent notices as the same question asked over and over. Blurred or double vision lasting more than five minutes also means an emergency room. So does an injury at high speed, such as a car crash, and so does a cut deep enough to need many stitches.
Paragraph 3: A big soft lump on the scalp looks frightening and usually means very little. The scalp has a large blood supply and bleeds easily into the loose tissue beneath, so a small injury can raise a large swelling. The skull bone separates that swelling from the brain. If your child is acting normally, the lump itself is the next thing to judge. Size still counts, and swelling wider than one inch means calling your office's main number now, at any hour. Feel the skull under the swelling as well, because a dent in the bone means calling your office's main number too, whatever the lump looks like. The When To Call tab below covers the rest.

### suicide-concerns
Lead: Call 911 now if your child has attempted suicide in the past 24 hours, or is confused, not making sense, or showing the effects of drugs or alcohol. If your child has none of those signs but has threatened suicide or has a plan, go to an emergency room tonight. If you are frightened and none of those signs fit your child yet, call or text 988, the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline, at any hour.
Paragraph 1: A plan means your child has a method in mind, such as taking drugs or using a weapon. If your child attempted suicide earlier than the past 24 hours but within the past week, go to an emergency room tonight. An emergency room is also right if your child has needed a psychiatric hospital before for symptoms like these. Do not leave your child alone. Call us at any hour.
Paragraph 2: Making the house safer is the next thing to do, and firearms come first, because firearms cause most suicide deaths. Move any firearms out of the house to a relative or a friend for now. If a firearm stays in the house, unload it and lock it away. Then go through whatever else your child could reach, starting with medicines and alcohol. The Care Advice tab below has the rest of the home safety plan.
Paragraph 3: Ask your child directly whether they are thinking of killing themselves. Plain words are safer than careful ones, and asking the question does not put the idea in your child's head. Listen without arguing. Do not promise to keep what your child tells you secret, and say that you are getting help because you love them. Thoughts of suicide, thoughts of seriously harming themselves, and themes of death in what your child draws or writes all need attention now. Call us and we will help you work out what comes next. For care that continues past tonight, our behavioral health services take children who are already patients at Wasatch Pediatrics. Call or text 988 at any hour, whether or not your child is a patient here.

### constipation
Paragraph 1: Your child is constipated if passing a stool hurts or brings on crying, if straining for more than ten minutes produces nothing, or if three or more days pass with no stool. Breastfed babies are the exception. A breastfed baby over a month old can go four to seven days between stools without being constipated, as long as those stools stay soft and painless. A breastfed baby under a month old is different, and needs a call to us now, at any hour, because a newborn who rarely stools may not be getting enough breast milk.
Paragraph 2: Any child who is not breastfed and goes four or five days almost always has pain and hard straining when the stool finally comes, and that pain is the reason to hold on again. Pain is one trigger. A power struggle is the other, and it is the most common cause of repeated constipation. The struggle usually starts over toilet training, and in a child who is already trained it often begins with the start of school, because some children refuse to use public toilets. Pressure and punishment make the holding back worse.
Paragraph 3: Go straight to an emergency room if your child's vomit is green, meaning the green of bile rather than the yellow of stomach juice. Call us now, at any hour, for belly or rectal pain that lasts more than an hour, for vomiting twice or more with a swollen belly, or for a new weak suck in a baby under a year old. The When To Call tab below has the rest. Otherwise constipation starts at home, with changes to food and a calmer routine, and the Care Advice tab below covers them. Call us before you start a stool softener, so that we can go over which one suits your child and how much to give.
