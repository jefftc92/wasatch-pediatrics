# Draft 08 — triage check (pre-apply)

editorial-triage read all six pages as 44 specific children. Ten findings, three
of them hold-the-draft. The three would each have reached a parent.

## Hold the draft

### 1. croup tells a stridor child to go to bed. Rule 15, and a collision with the page's own 911 line

Paragraph 3: "Then settle your child and listen again, and judge by what you
hear after the mist rather than before it. A barking cough with no stridor is
ordinary croup, and a child a year old or older with none of the signs above can
stay at home."

Paragraph 1: "If you can still hear stridor while your child is calm and
resting, call 911, whatever your child's age."

A three-year-old with loud stridor at rest at 11pm whose noise settles after the
shower matches both. Paragraph 3 explicitly instructs the parent to prefer the
later observation, so the lower branch is the one the page recommends. The
source puts stridor that has resolved at Contact Doctor Within 24 Hours, and
reserves self-care for a child who never had it.

New in this draft, introduced while satisfying rule 18's one concrete action.
Standing question 9 again.

### 2. suicide-concerns reopens the fixed fault three paragraphs lower

The lead now gates 988 correctly behind the 911 and ER signs. Paragraph 3 then
offers "Call us now, at any hour, if your child has thoughts of suicide... You
can call or text 988 instead" with no gate at all. Every child in the ER tier has
thoughts of suicide. A fifteen-year-old who said tonight they want to die and has
been counting out pills matches the ER in the lead and a phone number in
paragraph 3, and paragraph 3 is where a scanning parent lands cold.

The same shape as the fault the draft was written to fix, moved down the page.

### 3. suicide-concerns lead, sentence 3 is ungated, and our own escalation makes it bite

Sentence 2 carries its gate ("but not in the past 24 hours"). Sentence 3 does
not. A child who attempted three hours ago and also has a plan matches 911 and
"go tonight". Worse, raising drugs or alcohol from ER to 911 means a child who
attempted two days ago and is drunk now fires sentences 1 and 2 with different
instructions. Under the source they are one thing. Rule 19a exactly: raising one
branch made it overlap a neighbour, and it did not exist before this draft.

## Partial tiers, rule 21, on five of six pages

The systemic finding. Every page volunteers some items from the call-now tier and
drops others, and the reader treats what we chose to say as the complete set.

- **fever** carries 104°F and under-12-weeks, drops trouble breathing, great
  trouble swallowing, shaking chills over 30 minutes, nonstop crying, refusing to
  move a limb, dehydration, pain passing urine, weak immune system, looks very
  sick. Then it tells the parent the tab holds "the fevers that need a call
  within a day", which misdescribes the tier. A two-year-old at 101°F who is
  drooling and cannot swallow her spit matches no route on the page.
- **croup** drops fever over 104°F from a tier it otherwise carries four items
  from. A six-month-old with a croupy cough and 105°F gets only the 24-hour rule.
- **head-injury** now carries the age rule round 7 lost, confirmed, but drops
  "fall from a dangerous height", "blow from a hard object" and "bleeding that
  will not stop after 10 minutes". A three-year-old who fell from a second-storey
  window, up and playing with a small lump, matches nothing.
- **diaper-rash** keeps yellow scabs and drops spreading redness from the
  under-one-month tier. A three-week-old with spreading redness and no fever
  matches nothing. Carried over from live text, not a regression.
- **constipation** gives no rule for a baby under two months who is not
  breastfed, then sweeps them into "Otherwise constipation clears at home", with
  a diet step beginning "If your child eats solid food".
