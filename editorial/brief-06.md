# Revision brief 06 — six symptom pages

Round 5: three pages passed the reader cleanly (diaper-rash, head-injury,
suicide-concerns) and the suicide lead was called the best writing in the set.
Three pages come back. Both evaluators failed the round.

## Critical: two urgency downgrades

The triage gate missed both of these because it scored "the same day" as equal
to "now". It has been fixed and now flags them.

1. **fever.** "Above 104°F, call us the same day even if your child seems well."
   The AAP puts a fever higher than 104°F under **Call Doctor or Seek Care Now**.
   At midnight "the same day" means the morning. Route it now, at any hour, which
   is what the same paragraph already says for a baby under 3 months.
2. **constipation.** "Other signs mean calling us the same day." All three signs
   named after it sit in the same AAP call-now tier. Same fix.

Also on fever: "Above 104°F" leaves 104.0 undefined, and the reader was holding
a thermometer reading exactly that. Write 104°F or higher.

## Rule 19, and this time sweep for it

Rule 19 says collapse a tier split the reader cannot make while it is happening.
I applied it only to the sign it was written about, and the reader found two
more. Fix these two, then **go through all six pages and find the rest
yourself**, and list what you collapsed in your notes.

3. **croup.** Paragraph 1: stridor heard while your child is calm means severe
   croup, be seen tonight. Paragraph 2: constant and severe stridor means 911.
   The reader's child was sitting still and audibly breathing in: "Those sound
   like the same thing described twice with different answers. I read both twice
   and still don't know whether to dial 911 or get my coat." Collapse upward.
   Stridor you can hear while the child is calm and at rest means 911, and warm
   mist is what you do while help is coming.
4. **fever.** 911 for a child who "cannot be woken", emergency room if "hard to
   wake". "My child is groggy and floppy and takes a lot of shaking. I'm being
   asked to grade my own child on a distinction I can't make." Collapse upward.

## Placement, not wording

5. **constipation.** A parent of a breastfed two-month-old read "three or more
   days pass with no stool", concluded their child was constipated, then read
   through the emergency room sign, green bile, two tab pointers and the stool
   softener instruction before reaching "four to seven days is normal for that
   baby" at the very bottom. Both evaluators flagged it. Move the exception
   alongside the threshold it qualifies, in paragraph 1.

## Croup, the mist routine, where I am overruling evaluator A again

Evaluator A says the warm mist block reproduces AAP First Aid for Stridor and
must go. The round 4 reader failed the page for withholding the next step
mid-crisis, and the AAP itself says all stridor needs treating with warm mist.
It stays. But A is right about two things, so fix these:

- **The order is wrong.** Two treatment steps come before "Call us either way",
  when stridor heard now is a call-now sign. Put the routing first, then the mist
  as what to do while waiting.
- **The page never names the Care Advice tab at all.** Name it, once, for the
  rest of the home care.

Also: "Call us either way" has three candidate pairs in reach and the reader
could not tell which. And the page gives three separate instructions to call, so
the reader was skimming by the third, which is the one carrying the age split.
Consolidate.

## Diaper rash, where I am partly overruling evaluator A

A says the creams paragraph transcribes Care Advice and that product names break
rule 17. Keep **Lotrimin AF**: the round 4 reader failed the page for sending
them to a shelf of nine tubes without naming one, and rule 18 licenses exactly
this. Drop **Vaseline and Desitin** and say a protective ointment, since the
reader never asked for those and two brand names in one paragraph is the panel's
job. Reword "three days of warm-water cleaning and air exposure" so it is not
the panel's phrase verbatim.

Also: "Three days is also the point at which a cream starts to matter" reads as
either start a cream now or a cream becomes diagnostically relevant, and the next
sentence says creams mostly are not needed.
