# Revision brief — packet 1, round 1

Consolidated from seven independent reviews. Conflicts between reviewers are
resolved here, by the priority order: meaning and accuracy, logic, clarity,
natural language, directness, usefulness, readability, search intent, SEO,
polish. Do not re-litigate a resolution; act on it.

---

## A. Safety and accuracy. Fix these before anything else.

These came from the reader, not the editors. Each is a place where a parent
could act on a wrong understanding.

**A1. fever — the newborn threshold is missing.** The page says "any fever in a
baby under three months" and never says what a fever is at that age. A parent
holding a 99.8 does not know whether they are holding one. Restore 100.4°F,
which the AAP panel states and which this page dropped. Rule 12: it was lost
while removing care advice.

**A2. fever — "same day" contradicts the 24-hour line.** Two sentences after
offering a nurse at any hour, the page tells a parent of a feverish newborn to
call "the same day", which reads as "this can wait until morning". For a baby
under three months it cannot. Separate that instruction from the other two
triggers and make its urgency unambiguous.

**A3. constipation — the page has no red flags at all.** Every other page in
the packet tells a parent when to call the same day or call 911. This one
describes a months-long treatment and stops. A parent whose child has blood, a
hard distended belly, vomiting, or severe pain cannot tell from this page
whether that is part of the cycle being described. Add the escalation.

**A4. head-injury — "a significant height" gives no threshold.** The other two
emergency triggers on that line are observable. This one asks a frightened
parent to guess, and they will guess low. Give a figure, or describe it in
terms a parent can apply.

**A5. croup — the steroid has no route.** The page says a single dose usually
settles croup within hours and that we would rather give it. It never says how
a parent gets it at 2am. Say what happens after the call.

**A6. suicide-concerns — the lead shuts out most of its readers.** "Call 988 now
if your child has talked about ending their life" gates the page behind a
condition many parents arriving here do not meet: they found something on a
phone, or their child has stopped eating and will not look at them. Keep 988
first. Widen the condition.

## B. Logic and referents. All six pages.

**B1.** Name every referent. Reported on all six pages, most critically:
  - croup: "produces that sound in children who do not have it" — "it" could be
    the sound, the tightening, or croup.
  - suicide-concerns: "our behavioral health team can pick it up from there" —
    no antecedent, and office-casual in the one place it cannot be.
  - suicide-concerns: "Say the words rather than talking around them" — "the
    words" arrives only in the next sentence.
  - suicide-concerns: "them" collides across adjacent sentences, once meaning
    the words and once the child.
  - constipation: "Constipation is about how hard and how painful, not how
    often" — hard and painful *what*. The sentence does not complete.
  - constipation: "Stopping the moment things improve" — stopping what.
  - diaper-rash: "Call us if the rash matches that second description" — makes
    the reader count backwards through a paragraph.

**B2.** Two sentences fail a literal reading and must be rebuilt, not patched:
  - constipation: "Crossed legs, going stiff and hiding behind the sofa look
    like a child straining to go, and are almost always a child working hard
    not to." Behaviors are not a child.
  - head-injury: "Watch for repeated vomiting, ... or a child who is simply not
    themselves." Five signs then a child; "watch for a child" does not hold.

**B3.** head-injury: one "because" clause explains only the second of two
claims, and explains a forehead by naming the scalp.

## C. Repetition. The dominant fault in this packet.

Four of six pages state their thesis in the heading, restate it in the lead,
and restate it again in the first sentence of paragraph 1. head-injury does it
four times, twice ending on the same noun.

Keep the heading. It is fixed and it does the work. Cut the restatements.

  - head-injury: cut the lead and the P1 opener and "Size is not the signal."
    Keep the swelling sentence, which explains rather than asserts.
  - fever: the claim appears four times. Keep the heading and the paragraph 2
    version, which says what we do and is the one the panel cannot supply.
  - croup: cut "Croup sounds far worse than it usually is". The lead just said
    it. Keep the misleads-every-parent half, which adds something.
  - constipation: the hold-on cycle is stated in the lead and again in P1. Keep
    the P1 version, which continues into new material.
  - suicide-concerns: P1 currently re-issues the 988 instruction before adding
    the escalation. Lead with the escalation. Keep 988 in both places.

## D. Structure of lists

**D1.** head-injury: six red flags in one or-chain. Two sentences, all six
preserved. Losing one is a safety failure, not a tidy-up.
**D2.** fever: three "about a fever that..." limbs on one verb. Split. A1 and A2
require the newborn trigger to stand alone anyway.
**D3.** diaper-rash: four diagnostic features in one chain, in the sentence a
parent compares against a real rash on a phone.

## E. Hidden actors

Five of six pages state what this practice does in a construction that omits
the practice. head-injury is the exception and the model: "we will examine your
child and tell you exactly what to watch for overnight."

  - diaper-rash: "Yeast needs an antifungal cream" — a parent may read this as
    an instruction to go and buy one. Say who prescribes it.
  - croup: "A single dose of steroid usually settles croup" — see A5.
  - constipation: "a laxative dose matched to your child's age and weight" —
    matched by whom.
  - suicide-concerns: see B1.

## F. Register and word choice

  - constipation: "signalling" → signaling. Fails the automated gate. Rule 11,
    and the fourth recurrence of this fault in the project.
  - constipation: "sofa" → couch. "commonest" → most common. "straight back".
  - suicide-concerns: "straight away" → right away.
  - head-injury: "the knock" is British register.
  - croup: "we would far rather" is British register.
  - fever: "A child who manages all three" — children do not manage questions.
  - diaper-rash: "answers to a thick barrier paste" — rashes do not answer to
    things. "throws small spots out" is not how a rash is described.
  - diaper-rash: the same product is called a zinc oxide barrier, a barrier
    paste, barrier care and a barrier in four consecutive sentences.

## G. Search intent

**G1. diaper-rash lead is the highest-priority single fix in the packet.** It
opens with home treatment, which the AAP Care Advice tab carries in full, under
a heading that asks for a distinction. Rule 5 and rule 4. The lead this page
should have is sitting one sentence below: "Two different rashes wear the same
name, and they need different treatment."

**G2.** Two meta descriptions promise the panel's material as ours: croup's
"what helps at home" and diaper-rash's "the barrier cream and changing routine
that clears most of it". A searcher clicks the promise and lands on a page that
deliberately does not carry it.

**G3.** constipation: the heading asks why it takes months; the lead answers
what counts as constipated. The answer to the heading is two paragraphs away.

## H. Deliberate overrides. Do not "fix" these.

  - suicide-concerns puts 988 before the heading's question. Correct. A parent
    in crisis gets the number first.
  - croup's "worse at night" and "peaks on the second or third night" are two
    different facts. Both stay.
  - Imperatives across all six pages are correct and must survive unchanged.
    Rule 10.

## I. Cross-page

Three of six headings run "X, not Y". Each is individually right and none
should be weakened to break the pattern, but do not add a fourth.

"At whatever hour" appears four times across three pages. It is a constructed
formality. "Any time, day or night" is the spoken form.

Every page's paragraph 2 opens with "Call us" or "Call". A parent reading a
second page will skim it. Vary where the call instruction sits.
