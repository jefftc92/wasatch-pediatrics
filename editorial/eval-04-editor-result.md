# Round 4 blind evaluation A (senior editor) — FAIL

Verified every threshold, age and tier against the six cached AAP pages.

## Critical

1. **suicide-concerns, the lead.** "If you are worried your child may be suicidal
   and there has been no attempt, call or text 988." The AAP puts making threats
   and having a plan under **Go to ER Now**, and neither involves an attempt. A
   parent whose child has just described a plan meets this sentence's condition
   exactly and is routed to a phone line. The page's own next paragraph
   contradicts it. Less urgent than the source, in the most-read sentence on the
   page.
2. **constipation.** "Green vomit is the rare exception. Everything else starts
   at home." False against the source, which puts stomach or rectal pain lasting
   more than an hour, vomiting twice or more with a swollen belly, a breastfed
   baby under a month, and new weak suck under 12 months in Call Doctor or Seek
   Care Now. The paragraph then refutes itself four sentences later with "the
   When To Call tab below covers belly pain."
3. **croup.** "Lips that turn blue during a coughing fit are not safe either, and
   neither is stridor you can hear right now... Call your office's main number
   for either sign." Three faults in two sentences. "Not safe either" attaches to
   three consecutive emergency-room sentences, so the parent must reverse a
   triage decision mid-paragraph. "Are not safe" is padding where a route
   belongs. And the stated reason covers only one of the two signs, while telling
   the parent to phone and simultaneously asserting phone advice is inadequate.
4. **diaper-rash.** "An ordinary irritant rash needs no cream at all" hardens the
   source past what it says and contradicts a named exception in it: "If your
   child has diarrhea and a rash around the anus, use a protective ointment.
   Examples are Vaseline or Desitin." A parent with the commonest irritant rash
   of all is told the opposite of the panel below.

## Editorial

- croup: the mist routine, its burn caution and its fallback step are a near
  transcription of Care Advice, followed by a pointer to the tab that says the
  same thing. Rule 18 licenses one concrete action, not the whole routine.
- fever: "Take the temperature rectally in a baby under 6 months, because ear
  thermometers are not accurate before that age." The reason eliminates one
  alternative and does not establish the instruction. The source accepts a
  digital forehead reading and rules out only ear under 6 months and forehead
  strips at any age.
- head-injury: "Two things about a lump do change what we do... So does a dent
  you can feel in the skull." A dent is not a thing about a lump, and the
  paragraph has spent four sentences establishing lumps as reassuring. The
  framing blunts the sign that most needs to land.
- constipation: "Pressure and punishment make the holding back worse, so a child
  who is being pushed holds on harder." The clause after "so" restates the clause
  before it.
- suicide-concerns: the firearms, medicines, alcohol and car keys sequence
  reproduces the AAP Home Safety Plan point for point, in its order, with its
  rationale. Four bullets, four clauses.
- fever, meta description: "why any fever under 3 months means calling us
  tonight" attaches the age to the fever rather than the baby. The prose gets it
  right; the description is what appears in search results.
