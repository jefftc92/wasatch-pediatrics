# Revision brief 07 — six symptom pages

Round 6 is the closest yet. Every page answered the reader's question. Evaluator
A found no faults at all on head-injury or constipation prose, and recorded the
triage work as sound. Both evaluators still failed the round.

Three new rules: **19a**, **20**, and an amendment to **18**. Read them first.

## Critical

1. **croup, a collision our own escalation created.** "If you can still hear
   stridor while your child is calm and resting, call 911" collides with "A baby
   under a year old with any stridor at all needs an emergency room now." An
   infant with stridor at rest matches both and gets two different instructions.
   Resolve it: any stridor at rest is 911 regardless of age, and the infant rule
   covers stridor that comes only with crying. Then apply rule 19a to whatever
   you write, by reading the page as the specific child.
2. **diaper-rash, two uniqueness claims.** "The exception is a rash around the
   anus during diarrhea" then "A yeast rash is the one that needs a cream." Both
   cannot be the exception.
3. **diaper-rash, the paragraph loops.** The three-day rule and its yeast
   conclusion are given at the start, interrupted, then restated at the end as if
   new. Say it once. Move the diarrhea ointment out of the middle of the
   cream decision, where the reader is trying to choose.

## Rule 20, the structural one

4. **fever and croup both split their 911 items across a paragraph break.** The
   reader: "If I'd stopped at the end of the first paragraph, thinking that was
   the emergency list, I'd have missed the purple spots." Put every 911 item in
   one uninterrupted run, then the emergency-room items, then the rest.
5. **head-injury prints the same four signs twice, word for word**, once for now
   and once for resolved. The reader reported this in round 5 and again in round
   6, and thought the page had duplicated itself. Name the four signs once, then
   split by timing in the same breath.
6. **Stop counting items out loud.** "Three other signs mean calling us now" on
   constipation. "In all three cases" on croup, which made the reader scroll back
   and count on their fingers.

## Factual

7. **fever, "has stopped moving".** The AAP sign is "not moving or too weak to
   stand", a state. Ours requires the parent to have witnessed a change, so a
   child who has lain limp all afternoon falls outside it. Restore the state.
8. **suicide-concerns, overlapping windows.** "In the past 24 hours" at 911 and
   "at any point in the past week" at the emergency room. The second contains the
   first. "Earlier than that" fixes it.
9. **suicide-concerns, rule 19 candidate the sweep missed.** A child who is drunk
   and slurring fits both "confused and not making sense" at 911 and "using drugs
   or alcohol and showing the effects right now" at the emergency room. The
   reader: "In this situation, of all situations, I should not be sorting two
   similar descriptions into two different phone calls." Collapse upward, then
   apply rule 19a.

## Placement and voice

10. **head-injury, "Your child can sleep."** The thing the reader came for, and
    the meta description promises it, sitting in the last third of the third
    paragraph behind two paragraphs of emergency lists. Get it into the lead.
    Keep the fuller sleep guidance where it is.
11. **fever, the 104 reversal needs its reason.** The page argues the number is
    not what to watch, then gives a number that overrides the child, with "even
    if your child seems well". Give the reason, or a parent who believed the
    first half has grounds to discount the instruction.
12. **fever, the three questions arrive three times**: meta description, lead,
    then paragraph 1 one by one. Cut the duplication.
13. **fever, the medicine question does not apply to a young baby**, and the
    reader learns three sentences later they must not give it. "For a moment I
    wondered if I'd already done something wrong."
14. **constipation, "Every other child"** was read as every second child. Say any
    child who is not breastfed.
15. **constipation, paragraph 1 is one wall** carrying the definition, the
    breastfed exception, a newborn call-now, the holding cycle, toilet training
    and school. The newborn red flag sits mid-paragraph where a scanner slides
    past it. Split it.
16. **head-injury, "may have a mild concussion, and that also means an emergency
    room."** Called mild, then sent to the ER. "Which am I meant to feel?"
17. **croup, "while help is on the way"** is false where the parent has just
    phoned the office.
18. **Four consecutive sentences hung on "as well", "as well", "too"** on fever,
    and the same tic opens croup's and head-injury's second paragraphs.
19. **suicide-concerns, "which can happen after taking something"** is evasive on
    an otherwise plain page, and attaching one cause to a 911 sign invites a
    parent whose child took nothing to discount it.

## Do not lose these

- The croup lead, called the best on the site.
- "Asking the question does not put the idea in your child's head", called the
  single most useful sentence across all six pages.
- "Your child can sleep", and head-injury paragraph 3's explanation of why a
  lump is usually nothing.
- "Two different rashes wear the same name."
- The constipation holding-back explanation.
