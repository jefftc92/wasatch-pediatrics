# Draft 08 — panel check (pre-apply)

## It rejected my premise, correctly

I flagged "pears, prunes or peas" as generalizing a list the source scopes to
babies. Wrong. The AAP has two diet blocks, and peas, pears and prunes appear
verbatim in **both**:

- Infants under 1: "peas, beans, apricots, prunes, peaches, pears, or plums"
- Children over 1: "peas, beans, broccoli, bananas, apricots, peaches, pears,
  figs, prunes, or dates"

The draft took the intersection, which is the right move for one sentence that
has to serve a nine-month-old and a five-year-old at 11pm. Both fixes I proposed
would have made the page worse. Only the frequency is age-scoped, and that is
Care Advice detail the tab holds.

It then found the real fault in the same sentence, in the opposite direction:
"**If your child eats solid food**" names a class of reader and gives that class
nothing. The source has an action for exactly that parent, pear or apple juice
for a baby over a month old not yet on solids, and our conditional fences them
out of the only action sentence on the page.

## The finding both checkers reached independently

**croup P3 clears a child home against a subset of the tier.** "A child a year
old or older with none of the signs above can stay at home." The AAP call-now
tier has fourteen items; the page prints five. A three-year-old with a barking
cough, no stridor, a 105 fever and a neck that will not bend forward has "none
of the signs above" and is told to stay home.

Handing a tier to the panel is rule 17 working. Ruling that tier out on the
reader's behalf, from the subset we chose to print, is not. Contrast head-injury
P3, which uses the identical construction and then keeps going into the call-now
items and never closes the door.
