# Revision brief 04 — six symptom pages

Both evaluators failed round 3, independently, on different halves of the same
mistake. Every factual claim below was verified against /tmp/aap before it was
written into this brief.

## Two errors this brief inherits, and corrects

**Mine, and the more serious.** Brief 03 told you that the source's "public
toilets" excludes "daycare, travel and relatives' houses." That was my
inference, not the source. The AAP says only "some children refuse to use public
toilets" and, for a trained child, "it may begin with the start of school." You
followed the brief and produced "at daycare, at school, at a relative's house or
on a trip," which invents three specifics, and a relative's house is not a public
toilet. Write what the source says. If a brief asks you past the source again,
refuse it and say so, as the round 2 rewrite did.

**Rule 17, applied too far, which is also mine.** I told you to hand the lower
tiers to the panel and you did, correctly. The numbers went with them, and that
was not the intent. Rule 18 is new, it bounds rule 17, and you should read it
before starting. A threshold the judgement depends on is not a lower tier. It is
the judgement, and it stays on the page.

## The reader could not find what they came for

Four pages, verified afterwards against the meta descriptions, which promise
material the page no longer carries.

1. **fever.** A parent arrives holding a reading. The page never says what counts
   as a fever. Give 100.4°F rectally for a young baby. The description promises
   "what counts as a fever in a young baby."
2. **constipation.** The page explains why children hold back and never says what
   counts as constipated. Give the source's definition: pain or crying passing a
   stool, straining more than ten minutes without success, or three or more days
   without a stool. The description promises it.
3. **head-injury.** "One is how wide the swelling has spread, and the When To
   Call tab below gives the width that matters." Give the width. Larger than one
   inch. The reader has a hand on the lump.
4. **croup.** The description promises what to do in the next twenty minutes and
   the page offers nothing to do. Name one concrete step from Care Advice, then
   point at the tab for the rest.

Also, head-injury's "a dent you can feel in the skull" has no verb after it. Say
what to do about a dent.

## Critical factual faults

5. **diaper-rash.** "The two rashes need different creams" is wrong. The source
   treats ordinary irritant rash with warm-water cleansing and air exposure and
   no cream at all. Only yeast gets a cream, over the counter, no prescription,
   and that is the practical fact a parent wants at 11pm.
6. **diaper-rash.** "Its redness runs deeper" replaced the source's "bright red"
   with something no parent can assess at a changing table. Restore the visible
   cue.
7. **constipation.** "the exception to all of this" reads as exempting a
   breastfed six-week-old from the green-vomit emergency in the same paragraph.
   Bound the exception to stool frequency.
8. **head-injury.** Restore the dropped half of the ER sign: "cannot remember
   what happened **or store new memories**." A child asking the same question
   over and over is what a parent actually sees.
9. **head-injury.** "confused talk, slurred speech, unsteady walking or weakness
   that has now passed off" leaves the qualifier attached to weakness alone, so a
   currently confused child can be routed to the ER instead of 911. Make the
   timing distinction carry all four signs, in plain words. "Passed off" is not
   American usage, and one reader read it as "passed out", the opposite.

## The contradiction the reader hit hardest

10. **fever.** Paragraph 1 asks whether the child plays "once fever medicine has
    taken the edge off." Paragraph 2 says "Hold the fever medicine until we have
    seen your baby." The reader had to scroll back to discover the second applies
    only under 12 weeks. At 2am they may take the wrong one. Attach the age to
    the instruction, in the same sentence.

11. **croup.** "Lips or a face turning bluish when your child is not coughing
    mean 911." The reader asked whether blue lips during a coughing fit are
    therefore fine. Nothing on this page should leave that question open.

## Where the two evaluators disagree, and how to resolve it

Evaluator A says the head-injury watching and waking steps are Care Advice and
must go. The reader says "Your child can sleep" is the one thing they came for
and the sleep guidance is what the page exists to give. Rule 18 settles it:

- Keep "Your child can sleep." That is the judgement.
- Keep **one** action: wake them once, two hours after they fall asleep, and
  check they know you and can talk and walk normally.
- Drop "watch closely for the first two hours" and "sleep in the same room."
  Those are the panel's. Dropping the first also removes the two colliding
  two-hour clocks that both evaluators tripped on in two consecutive rounds, and
  "Separately" goes with it.

Same shape on constipation. The mechanism is ours. The list of rewards is Care
Advice. Keep at most one sentence saying pressure backfires, as the conclusion of
the explanation rather than as an instruction, and drop the praise and treats
list.

## Structure

12. **head-injury paragraph 1** is 210 words and twelve red flags in one block,
    on the page whose whole job is scanning at 2am. Break it by urgency: what
    means 911, then what means an emergency room. Two short paragraphs beat one
    long one here.
13. **constipation** has no bridge from green vomit to diet. Say the relationship:
    green vomit is the rare exception, everything else starts at home.
14. **suicide-concerns.** "Then go through the house" follows a sentence about
    who is eligible for a service, so nothing happened for "then" to follow. Give
    the means-restriction advice its own opening.

## Language

- fever: "has gone too weak" is not idiomatic. "The reading looks unremarkable"
  is clinician's vocabulary; the lead already says "the number". "At whatever
  hour" should be "at any hour", which is what the rest of the site says.
- fever: three consecutive sentences opening "Go straight", "Go as well", "Go
  too". The variation is cosmetic and draws attention to itself.
- fever: "under 12 weeks" makes a parent who thinks in months do arithmetic. Give
  both, or give months.
- croup: with "or" the verb takes the nearer subject, so "means". "A face" takes
  an indefinite article to your own child's face.
- croup: the first body sentence repeats the heading and the lead. Three
  statements of one point in three lines. Advance it instead.
- suicide-concerns: "Best of all" is the register of a bonus feature.
- suicide-concerns: "symptoms now" after "drug or alcohol use" leaves the reader
  guessing which symptoms.
- suicide-concerns: "988, 911 and the emergency room are open to every family"
  plants a doubt the reader did not arrive with. Keep the fact, lose the framing.
- constipation: "meets pain and hard straining" is a phrase nobody says.
- diaper-rash: "not only in newborns" answers an objection the page never raised.

## Do not lose these

Named by the evaluators as the writing that works:

- "Ask your child directly whether they are thinking of killing themselves...
  asking the question does not put the idea in your child's head." Called the
  best writing across all six pages.
- "The skull bone separates that swelling from the brain."
- "Your child can sleep."
- "Two different rashes wear the same name." The only lead of the six understood
  on the first pass without effort.
- "Call us before you start a stool softener, so that we can go over which one
  suits your child and how much to give."
- The three questions that open the fever page.
