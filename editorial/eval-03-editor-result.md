# Round 3 blind evaluation A (senior editor) — FAIL

Checked against the AAP sources. The three critical factual claims were verified
directly in /tmp/aap before this file was written, and all three hold.

## Critical, verified

1. **diaper-rash** — "The two rashes need different creams, and the Care Advice
   tab covers both." The source treats ordinary irritant rash with warm-water
   cleansing and air exposure and **no cream at all**. A cream appears only for
   yeast, plus a barrier ointment in the diarrhea case. The sentence also
   contradicts this page's own paragraph 1. A parent goes looking for two tubes
   and finds one.
2. **diaper-rash** — "Its redness runs deeper." The source's cue is **"bright
   red"**, an intensity a parent can see. "Runs deeper" asserts depth into the
   skin, which the source does not say and which nobody can judge at a changing
   table. The one usable distinguishing cue was replaced with an unusable one.
3. **constipation** — "Breastfed babies over a month old are the exception to all
   of this." Read literally this exempts a breastfed six-week-old from the
   paragraph's own emergency instruction about green vomit. The source's
   exception is narrow: stool frequency, when stools are soft and painless.
4. **head-injury** — "confused talk, slurred speech, unsteady walking or weakness
   that has now passed off." The qualifier attaches to "weakness" alone. The AAP
   splits these four by timing, present now being 911 and resolved being ER, and
   a narrow reading routes a currently confused child to the ER. An ambiguity
   that moves a triage tier. "Passed off" is also not American usage.

## High

5. **head-injury** — the AAP item is "Can't remember what happened **or store new
   memories**." The second half is dropped. Storing no new memories is what a
   parent actually observes, the child asking the same question over and over,
   and rule 17 requires every ER sign in full.
6. **constipation** — "at daycare, at school, at a relative's house or on a trip"
   are invented. The source says only "some children refuse to use public
   toilets" and, for a trained child, "it may begin with the start of school." A
   relative's house is not a public toilet.
7. **head-injury** — the watch, wake and same-room steps are transcribed
   near-verbatim from Care Advice. Only "Your child can sleep" is ours to say.

## Medium

  the tier rule 17 names explicitly as not ours to carry.
- head-injury — paragraph 1 is twelve red-flag sentences, roughly 210 words, in
  one block. The page whose whole job is scanning at 2am is the least scannable
  of the six.
- constipation — "Pressure and punishment make holding back worse. Praise, treats
  and small rewards..." is Care Advice, transcribed.
- constipation — no bridge between the green-vomit emergency and the diet
  sentence that follows it.
- croup — "Lips or a face turning bluish... mean 911": with "or" the verb takes
  the nearer subject, so "means". "A face" also takes an indefinite article to
  your own child's face.
- croup — the first body sentence states the same point as the heading and the
  lead, three times in three lines.
- suicide-concerns — "Then go through the house" follows a sentence about who is
  eligible for a service. Nothing happened for "then" to follow.
- suicide-concerns — "Best of all" is the register of a bonus feature,
  introducing the most consequential act on the page. The source's "Better yet"
  is already plainer.
