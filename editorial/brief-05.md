## Paragraphs already drafted and agreed

These four are settled. Use them as written unless the source contradicts them,
in which case say so. Do not re-litigate them.

**croup, paragraph 1**

Stridor is the sound to listen for, and crying makes it louder, so comfort your
child first and listen again once they have settled. Stridor you can still hear
while your child is calm and resting means severe croup, and severe croup is a
911 call rather than something to treat at home. For milder stridor, start warm
mist. Run a hot shower in a closed bathroom and sit with your child in the mist
for twenty minutes, staying away from the water so that nobody gets burned. If
the mist has not helped by then and the night is cold, take your child outside
for a few minutes. Call us either way, because stridor means your child needs to
be seen rather than advised over the phone.

**croup, paragraph 2**

Call 911 if your child is struggling for each breath, if the stridor is constant
and severe, or if your child passes out or stops breathing. Call 911 for lips or
a face turning blue, whether or not your child is coughing at the time. Drooling,
spitting or great trouble swallowing means 911, and so does croup that started
suddenly after a bee sting, a new medicine, or a food your child is allergic to.
Go straight to an emergency room if your baby is under a year old and has
stridor. Go as well if the ribs pull in with each breath, or if your child may
have choked on a small object. In a child over a year old, stridor you can hear
right now means calling your office's main number at any hour.

**suicide-concerns, lead**

Call 911 now if your child has attempted suicide in the past 24 hours, or has
taken something and is confused or not making sense. Go to an emergency room
tonight if your child has threatened suicide or has a plan. If you are frightened
and none of that has happened yet, call or text 988, the Suicide and Crisis
Lifeline, at any hour.

**fever, the opening of paragraph 1**

Three questions settle most fevers at home. Is your child drinking? Do they
respond to you normally? Do they play a little once fever medicine has taken the
edge off? A child who does all three is usually well enough to watch at home,
even at 103. A high number is uncomfortable rather than dangerous, and the fever
itself is not damaging your child. Above 104°F, call us the same day even if your
child seems well. A true fever means 100.4°F or higher taken rectally, in the ear
or on the forehead, 100°F by mouth, or 99°F under the arm. Any of those readings
counts, though an ear thermometer is not reliable under 6 months, and forehead
strips are not reliable at any age.

Carry the rest of fever paragraph 1, the 911 routing, through unchanged in
substance.
